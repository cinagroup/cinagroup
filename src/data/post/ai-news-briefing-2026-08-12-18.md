## Top 7 Stories

**1. Google DeepMind's Biggest Leadership Shake-Up Yet: Hassabis Steps Back, Jeff Dean Exits**

Demis Hassabis is handing over the CEO role at Google DeepMind to Koray Kavukcuoglu, moving into a chief scientist position, according to multiple reports. The reshuffle comes as the lab loses four founding-era leaders in a single day — including Jeff Dean, who is leaving Alphabet after 27 years — and Google's stock dipped on the news.

Fortune reports the exits follow low morale, a talent exodus, and repeated model delays, with Geeky Gadgets and others saying Google has reportedly canceled Gemini 3.5 Pro to pivot resources toward a Gemini 4 launch. The new AI boss inherits a race to catch OpenAI and Anthropic at the worst possible moment: with Gemini at 1 billion users but momentum questioned, the pressure is on Kavukcuoglu and Hassabis to deliver a flagship win before the exodus deepens.

**2. Zuckerberg Lays Out Meta's New AI Vision: 'Personal Intelligence for All'**

Mark Zuckerberg has unveiled a new strategic vision for Meta in an LA Times interview: "personal intelligence for all" — AI systems built around individual users that know their context, habits, and goals. The vision is anchored by the launch of Glimmer, a new Meta AI model that TechCrunch says offers the first concrete hint of how personal AI will work, with Yahoo Finance noting Meta shares gained on the announcement.

Meta is repositioning from social AI assistants to deeply personalized, on-device intelligence — a bet that privacy, context, and memory beat raw benchmark scores. It also sets up a direct consumer-agent clash with Google, OpenAI, and Anthropic, with Zuckerberg arguing personalization will be the next great differentiator in AI.

**3. OpenAI's Daybreak Cyber Agents Launch on AWS — Making Offensive AI a Product**

OpenAI and AWS announced that Daybreak Red and Daybreak Blue — the offensive and defensive halves of OpenAI's new cyber-defense agent family — are now available to eligible customers on Amazon Bedrock. OpenAI's blog frames the release as "accelerating cyber defense," with the models designed to autonomously probe systems, find vulnerabilities, and harden defenses.

The launch marks offensive AI becoming a premium enterprise product category, as CNBC separately reports that AI agents' "alarming" hacking skills are driving a rush of cybersecurity spending. Putting Daybreak on AWS gives OpenAI instant enterprise distribution — and raises the stakes in the debate over autonomous offensive agents and who gets to deploy them.

**4. Taiwan Hit by Unprecedented 'Autonomous' AI Cyber Attack, Financial Times Reports**

The Financial Times reports that China-linked hackers launched an unprecedented "autonomous" AI cyber attack against Taiwan, with the AI systems reportedly selecting targets and executing intrusions with limited human direction. The attack is described as the first major real-world demonstration of AI-driven offensive operations at scale.

Security researchers say the incident marks a threshold moment: autonomous AI agents are no longer lab experiments but active weapons in state-level cyber conflict. Expect governments to accelerate both AI-powered defenses and international rules of the road for autonomous offensive systems — a conversation that just got much more urgent.

**5. Cognition in Funding Talks at $40 Billion Valuation**

Bloomberg reports that AI coding startup Cognition — maker of the Devin coding agent and backed by Peter Thiel's Founders Fund — is in talks to raise a new round that would value the company at roughly $40 billion. That would be a massive step up from its last reported valuation and one of the richest-ever rounds for an AI agent startup.

Cognition's surge underscores investor conviction that AI coding agents are the killer app of the agent era. With Devin positioned against GitHub Copilot, OpenAI Codex, and Anthropic's coding tools, a $40B price tag says the market believes autonomous software engineering will be one of AI's biggest businesses.

**6. Bank of America to Deploy $250 Billion to AI and Energy Infrastructure**

In an exclusive, the WSJ reports Bank of America is committing $250 billion to finance AI and energy infrastructure, joining the wave of institutional capital flooding into data centers, power, and grid projects. The commitment follows NVIDIA's $500B Wall Street financing consortium and reflects banks' growing conviction that AI capex is a durable, decades-long investment cycle.

The sheer scale of bank commitments raises both opportunity and risk: infrastructure financing is becoming a new asset class, but concentrated leverage in power-hungry data centers could amplify a downturn. Watch how much of this capital actually deploys versus gets announced.

**7. Foxconn's Q2 Profit Jumps 35% on AI Demand, Beating Forecasts**

Foxconn, the world's largest contract electronics maker and Apple's key manufacturing partner, reported a 35% rise in second-quarter profit on surging AI server demand, beating analyst forecasts. The results confirm that the AI buildout is translating into hard revenue for the supply chain, with AI servers now a core growth engine alongside iPhones.

Foxconn's numbers are a useful leading indicator for the broader AI trade: as long as hyperscaler capex holds, so does the manufacturing boom. Any wobble in AI orders would show up in Foxconn's results first — making the company a bellwether worth watching.
