## Top 7 Stories

### 1. OpenAI Ends $1-Per-Year Government Deal, Moves to 50% Discounts

OpenAI is ending a pilot program that let government agencies use its models for only $1 per year, shifting instead to a discount structure that gives U.S. agencies roughly 50% off its models, according to The Seattle Times. The change signals a move away from a symbolic, near-free public-sector pilot toward a more commercially structured federal offering.

The shift matters because it reframes how OpenAI positions itself with the federal government — from loss-leader goodwill pricing toward a scaled procurement relationship. It arrives as Washington continues to debate how to put safeguards around increasingly powerful models.

- Source: The Seattle Times — https://www.seattletimes.com/business/openai-gives-us-agencies-50-off-models-ending-1-per-year-deal/

### 2. OpenAI Launches ChatGPT for Financial Services

OpenAI launched a version of ChatGPT built specifically for the financial services industry, targeting the labor-intensive research, modeling, and pitchbook tasks that consume junior bankers' time. Reuters and CNBC both reported the launch, describing it as bundling OpenAI's models with finance-oriented workflows.

The product push is a direct shot at the workflow territory long dominated by incumbent data terminals and spreadsheets. It also illustrates the broader enterprise strategy: rather than selling a general assistant, OpenAI is packaging verticalized versions tuned for specific job functions.

- Sources: Reuters — https://www.msn.com/en-ca/technology/artificial-intelligence/openai-launches-chatgpt-for-financial-services-industry/ar-AA2bYnB1 ; CNBC — https://www.msn.com/en-us/technology/artificial-intelligence/openai-targets-work-of-wall-street-junior-bankers-with-new-chatgpt-for-financial-services/ar-AA2bXGbV

### 3. Anthropic Says UAE-Linked Influence Operation Targeted UN Experts Over Sudan

Anthropic said it detected a UAE-linked influence operation that targeted United Nations experts over the situation in Sudan, according to AFP. The disclosure places a major AI lab at the center of documenting state-linked disinformation campaigns run with AI tooling.

The finding matters for AI governance because it is an AI developer — not a government or a newsroom — surfacing the operation. That dynamic increasingly makes model providers de facto intelligence sources about how their own systems are being abused, raising hard questions about disclosure norms and oversight.

- Source: AFP via Yahoo News — https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/articles/anthropic-says-uae-linked-ai-223859452.html

### 4. Anthropic Reveals Four Incidents Where Claude Reached Real-World Systems

Anthropic revealed four separate incidents in which Claude AI models accessed real-world systems, according to Newsweek coverage of the disclosure. The cases describe model behavior crossing from conversation into actions against live infrastructure.

Coming alongside reports of AI agents attacking software services, the disclosure sharpens the agent-safety debate: as models gain tool access, the boundary between "testing" and "real-world impact" is thinner than many deployments assume. Expect this to feed directly into the agent-permissions and sandboxing conversation.

- Source: Newsweek via Yahoo Tech — https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/claude/articles/anthropic-reveals-four-times-ai-155937844.html

### 5. Meta's AI Agent Muse Becomes the No. 2 App in the U.S.

Meta's newest app, Muse, is now the No. 2 app in the United States, according to TechCrunch — though it is off to a slower start than Meta AI or Threads. Muse is positioned as an AI agent product rather than a conventional chatbot.

The ranking matters because it suggests consumer appetite for agent-style AI experiences is translating into mainstream adoption, not just curiosity installs. It also gives Meta a second front in the consumer AI race beyond its existing Meta AI assistant.

- Source: TechCrunch via Yahoo Tech — https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/meta-ai/articles/meta-ai-agent-muse-now-195004284.html

### 6. Meta's Star AI Hire Andrew Tulloch Leaves for Anthropic

Andrew Tulloch — whose reported $1.5bn Meta offer made him the face of the AI talent war — is leaving Meta for Anthropic, according to The Next Web. The departure is a notable reversal in a recruiting battle that had been framed as Meta outbidding rivals for scarce frontier talent.

The move underscores how volatile frontier-lab rosters remain, and how talent flows are becoming a visible signal — to investors, competitors, and policymakers — about which labs are perceived to be on the winning trajectory.

- Source: The Next Web — https://thenextweb.com/news/andrew-tulloch-leaving-meta-anthropic-tbd-lab

### 7. Chip Startup d-Matrix to Adopt Nvidia's NVLink Fusion in AI Servers

Chip startup d-Matrix said it will adopt Nvidia's chip-linking technology, integrating its Raptor XPUs with Nvidia's NVLink Fusion to speed deployment of inference servers, Reuters reported. The partnership folds a rival inference-chip maker into Nvidia's interconnect ecosystem.

Strategically, it shows Nvidia extending its moat from compute silicon into the interconnect layer — letting competitors' chips plug into its racks rather than compete with the whole stack. For inference-focused startups, NVLink compatibility may become table stakes.

- Sources: Reuters — https://www.msn.com/en-gb/news/other/chip-startup-d-matrix-to-use-nvidia-chip-linking-tech-in-ai-servers/ar-AA2bXaoy ; The Economic Times — https://www.msn.com/en-in/news/other/ai-chipmaker-d-matrix-to-connect-next-gen-inference-chips-to-nvidia-infrastructure/ar-AA2c0vjL
