## Top 7 Stories

### 1. Nvidia Unveils Vera Rubin AI Platform, Delivers to OpenAI and Anthropic

Nvidia introduced Vera Rubin, its next-generation seven-chip AI platform, and hand-delivered the first units to OpenAI, Anthropic, SpaceX, and Oracle. The announcement came amid Nvidia's staggering $68 billion quarterly earnings report, underscoring the company's dominance in AI compute. Elon Musk greeted the delivery with characteristic wordplay: "Vera nice, Vera nice."

The Vera Rubin platform represents a significant architectural leap, designed specifically for the compute demands of frontier AI models. With Meta also reportedly on board, Nvidia is cementing its role as the essential hardware partner for every major AI lab — even as it signals a strategic shift in how it engages with those same labs financially.

### 2. Jensen Huang Signals $30B OpenAI Investment "Might Be the Last"

In a striking pivot, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang told CNBC that the company's latest $30 billion investment in OpenAI "might be the last," confirming that Nvidia is pulling back from direct investments in AI labs. Combined with its stake in Anthropic, Nvidia has poured roughly $40 billion into funding the very customers that buy its chips — a circular arrangement Huang now appears ready to unwind.

The pullback raises broader questions about the sustainability of the AI funding model, where hardware providers effectively subsidize their own customers. Analysts note that as AI labs mature toward IPOs, strategic investors like Nvidia may prefer to cash out rather than double down.

### 3. SpaceX, OpenAI, and Anthropic Race Toward Public Markets

The AI industry's biggest private names are accelerating toward IPOs. SpaceX briefly dipped below its $135 IPO price this week, while OpenAI and Anthropic continue preparing their own public offerings. A new ProShares "FAB 10" ETF filing proposes bundling OpenAI, Anthropic, SpaceX, and Nvidia into a single tradable vehicle — Wall Street's latest attempt to package the AI boom for retail investors.

The question looming over all three: can public markets absorb the enormous valuations these companies command? With HPCwire framing the moment as "Can Wall Street Finance the AI Boom?", the coming quarters will test whether the IPO window stays open for AI's most ambitious players.

### 4. EU Orders Google to Open Android to AI Rivals

In a landmark regulatory ruling, the European Union ordered Google to give AI rivals greater access to Android smartphones and to share search data with competing AI services. The decision, reported by The New York Times and The Verge, could reshape the AI application ecosystem by forcing Google to loosen its grip on the dominant mobile platform.

The order targets Google's ability to preference its own AI services — like Gemini — on Android devices, potentially opening the door for alternative AI assistants and search experiences to compete on equal footing. Google is expected to appeal, but the direction of travel is clear: regulators view AI distribution control as the next antitrust frontier.

### 5. Pentagon Expands Classified AI Deals — Conspicuously Excludes Anthropic

The U.S. Department of Defense struck expanded classified AI agreements with eight companies, including OpenAI, Google, and Nvidia — but pointedly excluded Anthropic. The snub stems from an ongoing dispute between the Pentagon and Anthropic, reportedly centered on the company's reluctance to engage with military applications of its technology.

The Verge and DefenseScoop reported that the exclusion is not a technical judgment but a policy standoff. Anthropic has positioned itself as the safety-focused AI lab, and that branding now carries real-world consequences as defense contracts become a major revenue stream for its competitors. The Pentagon's message is unambiguous: AI labs that won't work with the military will be left out of the classified AI buildout.

### 6. Mistral Explores Building Its Own AI Chips

French AI startup Mistral is considering developing its own AI chips, according to Seeking Alpha, as it seeks to reduce dependence on Nvidia hardware while competing with OpenAI and Anthropic. The move would make Mistral one of the few AI labs pursuing vertical integration into silicon — a path that only the largest players have attempted.

The chip exploration signals growing frustration with the Nvidia-dominated supply chain, even as Nvidia delivers record volumes. For a European champion like Mistral, owning the hardware stack could also be a matter of strategic autonomy, aligning with broader EU "technological sovereignty" ambitions.

### 7. Palantir's Alex Karp Unleashes on AI Token Pricing

Palantir CEO Alex Karp launched a blistering critique of OpenAI and Anthropic's token-based pricing models, telling CNBC that "something has gone completely wrong" with how AI companies charge for their products. Karp argued that per-token billing creates perverse incentives and fails to capture the actual value AI delivers to enterprise customers.

The criticism lands at a sensitive moment: Nvidia's $68B quarter put "token economics at the center of R&D computing," and as enterprises scale their AI usage, pricing models built on per-token metering face growing scrutiny. Karp's broadside suggests the industry may be heading toward a pricing reckoning.
