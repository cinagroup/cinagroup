## Top 7 Stories

### 1. OpenAI, Anthropic and Google DeepMind Move Toward an Industry AI Safety Body

Representatives from OpenAI, Anthropic and Google DeepMind have been meeting regularly since July as a working group to build an industry-led AI safety standards body, according to reporting from The Information's coverage of the talks. The proposed body would institute pre-release audits of frontier models — a self-regulatory structure the three labs would largely design and govern themselves.

The move lands amid rising antitrust scrutiny of the same companies. Critics, including Cohere CEO Aidan Gomez, have characterized the joint safety push as a potential cover for competitive coordination rather than genuine self-restraint, per Gizmodo. Regulators in both the US and EU are already examining concentration in the frontier-model market, so a private standards body could either forestall public rules or invite accusations of regulatory capture.

### 2. Trump Rejects CEO Call to Slow AI: "Whoever Wins AI Wins"

President Trump publicly rejected a request from the CEOs of Anthropic, OpenAI and xAI to slow the pace of AI development, telling reporters that "whoever wins AI wins" — a framing that prioritizes competitive advantage over caution. House Speaker Mike Johnson responded to the same appeal by calling for meetings rather than committing to any slowdown.

The exchange follows a CNBC report that warnings of "extinction"-level risk are intensifying as more OpenAI and Anthropic researchers join public calls for an AI slowdown. The contrast between lab leadership asking for restraint and the political leadership declining it is now the defining tension in US AI governance.

### 3. Anthropic's CEO Calls for a Global AI Slowdown — Rivals Agree

Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei called for a global AI slowdown, arguing "we owe it to humanity to try," and in a rare moment of unity Elon Musk, Sam Altman and Google's AI chief were reported to concur with the general thrust of the appeal. The alignment is notable given how frequently the same executives have clashed publicly over capability claims and safety positioning.

The essay also rattled public markets: Nvidia shares reportedly slipped as AI-spending worries grew in the wake of the slowdown argument, compounded by reporting of DOJ scrutiny. The episode shows that frontier-lab safety rhetoric now has an immediate pricing effect on the AI infrastructure trade.

### 4. Hackers Reportedly Used Claude to Breach OpenAI Systems in 72 Hours

A group operating as Hacktron AI reportedly used Anthropic's Claude to hack into OpenAI's internal systems in under 72 hours, exposing GitHub, Slack and Outlook-adjacent access, per CoinCentral. If accurate, the incident is a stark demonstration of agentic AI being repurposed as offensive tooling against another frontier lab.

The story reinforces a mounting concern among safety researchers: the same model capabilities that power useful agents also shorten the time-to-exploit for attackers. It also raises awkward questions for the coordinated safety-body effort described above, since any pre-release audit regime would need to cover misuse pathways, not just model behavior.

Source: https://coincentral.com/hackers-used-anthropics-own-ai-to-break-into-openai-and-it-only-took-72-hours/

### 5. Meta's Muse Agent Drives Stock Pop as Staff Flag Security Flaws

Meta launched Muse, a personal AI agent that can send email, book travel, shop, fill forms and continue working after the user closes the app — a consumer-facing step beyond the chatbot pattern. Shares reportedly rose about 5% on investor optimism following the introduction.

The launch was not frictionless: Reuters reporting cited by Forbes indicated Meta employees flagged security flaws in the agent, a recurring risk for systems granted payment and account authority. The company's broader enterprise AI push, which Zuckerberg says extends beyond agents, is now paired with a consumer agent whose trust model is still being stress-tested.

### 6. House Passes Ratepayer Protection Act Over Data Center Power Costs

The US House passed the Ratepayer Protection Act on September 16, 2026 by a 417–3 vote, requiring state utility regulators to address the electricity-cost burden that AI data centers place on ordinary customers. The bipartisan margin signals how politically potent data center energy backlash has become.

The vote coincides with local resistance in Silicon Valley itself, where residents and environmental groups in San Jose are organizing against proposed data center buildouts, per Reuters. With PwC projecting hundreds of billions in annual AI infrastructure spend through mid-century, power availability — and who pays for it — is becoming the binding constraint on the buildout.

Sources: https://www.unite.ai/house-passes-ratepayer-protection-act-on-data-center-power-costs/ · https://www.msn.com/en-us/news/other/in-silicon-valley-ai-data-center-boom-meets-local-resistance/ar-AA2cpX7q

### 7. Nvidia's Moat Tested From Both Directions: China Chips and Broadcom Growth

Nvidia faces a two-front competitive question. China's homegrown AI chip push has accelerated — with US export restrictions ironically benefiting domestic Chinese rivals — while Broadcom is projecting faster AI revenue growth off a smaller base, per Motley Fool analysis carried by Yahoo Finance.

Nvidia is responding on the capital side, committing $2 billion to Brookfield's AI infrastructure fund and investing $3.5 billion in MediaTek to stay central to AI hardware as large customers design their own silicon. The strategic bet is that financing and ecosystem position matter as much as raw accelerator performance.
