## Top 7 Stories

### 1. House Unveils Federal AI Draft Bill to Preempt State Laws
The U.S. House of Representatives has unveiled a sweeping draft AI bill that would establish a federal regulatory framework and preempt existing state-level AI laws. The legislation aims to create uniform standards for AI development and deployment across the country, replacing the current patchwork of state regulations. This move is being closely watched by major tech companies operating in multiple jurisdictions. The bill represents the most significant federal attempt yet to regulate artificial intelligence in the United States.

### 2. Meta Enters Enterprise AI Race With New Business Agents
Meta has officially launched its enterprise AI agent platform, aiming to help businesses automate customer service, sales, and internal operations. The new agents are designed to integrate with existing enterprise systems and can handle complex multi-step workflows. Bloomberg reports Meta is selling these AI agents directly to businesses in a push to diversify revenue beyond advertising. CEO Mark Zuckerberg envisions a future where Meta's AI agents run entire business operations autonomously, marking a strategic pivot toward the enterprise market.

### 3. Anthropic Files for IPO With $965B Valuation
Anthropic has officially filed for its U.S. stock market debut, reportedly valuing the company at $965 billion — surpassing OpenAI in market valuation expectations. The filing comes after Anthropic's Claude model gained significant market share in the enterprise AI segment. Analysts note the IPO could trigger a wave of AI company public listings, with OpenAI and SpaceX/xAI also preparing their own offerings. The company recently secured a compute deal with SpaceX and raised usage limits for Claude, signaling strong competitive positioning.

### 4. Broadcom Shares Plunge After AI Outlook Misses Investor Expectations
Broadcom shares dropped sharply after the chipmaker's AI revenue outlook fell short of Wall Street's high expectations. The selloff contributed to a broader cooling in the AI trade across equity markets, with investors recalibrating growth assumptions for AI hardware providers. Reuters reported that the AI rally is showing signs of fatigue as Asia rotates and sentiment shifts. The decline serves as a reminder that even the most successful AI-adjacent stocks face intense scrutiny in 2026.

### 5. Trump Administration Moves to Pre-Check OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic Models
The Trump administration has announced a new policy requiring pre-deployment safety reviews of AI models from OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic before they can be released to the public. The initiative aims to establish government oversight of frontier AI development, including assessments of bioweapon prevention capabilities and other safety concerns. Meta and other companies have reportedly united on bioweapon prevention protocols, even as regulatory pressure intensifies.

### 6. IBM and Google Cloud Announce Strategic AI Partnership
IBM and Google Cloud have announced a strategic partnership to scale AI capabilities by combining human expertise with AI-powered delivery. The collaboration will integrate Google's AI models with IBM's consulting and enterprise services, targeting large organizations looking to deploy AI at scale. The partnership signals growing consolidation in the enterprise AI market as cloud providers seek to differentiate through specialized offerings. IBM's Think 2026 conference has emphasized agentic AI as the next frontier in enterprise technology.

### 7. NVIDIA Introduces First PCs Designed for AI Agents
NVIDIA has unveiled a new line of personal computers specifically designed to run AI agents locally, representing CEO Jensen Huang's bid to win at every layer of the AI stack. The new chips enable on-device AI processing for autonomous agents, reducing reliance on cloud infrastructure. Separately, NVIDIA's Nemotron 3 Ultra has been recognized as America's best open AI model, though Chinese competitors continue to advance rapidly in the open-source space.
