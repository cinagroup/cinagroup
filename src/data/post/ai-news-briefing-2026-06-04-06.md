## Top 7 Stories

### 1. Alphabet Raises Record $85B for Google's AI Business

Alphabet has completed a record-breaking $85 billion capital raise dedicated to Google's AI infrastructure and research efforts. The unprecedented funding signals massive investor confidence in Google's AI strategy and its ambition to compete head-on with OpenAI and Anthropic in the race toward artificial general intelligence.

The funds will accelerate data center expansion, GPU procurement, and talent acquisition across Google DeepMind and other AI divisions. Analysts view this as one of the largest single capital commitments to AI development in history, underscoring the trillion-dollar potential of the technology.

### 2. Anthropic Scales Claude Mythos to Critical Infrastructure in 15+ Countries

Anthropic announced that its Claude Mythos model has been deployed to critical infrastructure operators across more than 15 countries. The model, designed for high-stakes decision-making in sectors such as energy, transportation, and telecommunications, represents Anthropic's push into enterprise-critical applications.

The expansion follows rigorous safety testing and alignment work, with Anthropic emphasizing constitutional AI principles. The move positions Claude as a trusted AI partner for national infrastructure systems, a domain where reliability and safety are paramount.

### 3. Trump Signs Narrower AI Executive Order After Industry Pushback

President Trump has signed a revised executive order on AI oversight that scales back earlier, more stringent provisions following significant pushback from the technology industry. The narrower order focuses on voluntary safety frameworks and interagency coordination rather than mandatory compliance requirements.

Industry leaders praised the revised approach, arguing that overly prescriptive regulation could stifle innovation and hand competitive advantages to China. The order still requires federal agencies to develop AI risk assessment guidelines, but companies will have greater flexibility in implementation.

### 4. Meta's WhatsApp Business AI Agent Now Available Globally

Meta has launched its AI-powered Business Agent for WhatsApp Business on a global scale. The agent can handle customer inquiries, process orders, and manage support tickets autonomously within WhatsApp's messaging interface, reaching over two billion users worldwide.

This marks a significant expansion of Meta's AI agent strategy, moving beyond research prototypes to production-scale deployment. Businesses can set up the agent with minimal configuration, potentially transforming how millions of companies interact with customers on the world's most popular messaging platform.

### 5. OpenAI Launches New Codex Tools for White-Collar Work

OpenAI has introduced a new suite of Codex tools designed to automate white-collar tasks including document analysis, spreadsheet management, and workflow orchestration. The tools extend OpenAI's coding-focused AI capabilities into broader professional productivity applications.

The launch signals OpenAI's ambition to move beyond developer tools into the mainstream enterprise software market. Early demonstrations showed Codex agents handling complex multi-step tasks across legal, finance, and operations workflows with minimal human oversight.

### 6. Google Rolls Out AI Deepfake Call Detection

Google has deployed a fake call detection feature across its services to protect users against AI-powered voice impersonation scams. The technology analyzes incoming calls for synthetic voice artifacts and deepfake indicators, alerting users in real time when a call appears to be generated by AI.

The feature comes as AI voice cloning scams have surged, with fraudsters using cloned voices of family members and executives to extract money and sensitive information. Google's detection system leverages advances in audio forensics and machine learning to identify synthetic speech patterns that are imperceptible to the human ear.

### 7. Microsoft Launches Scout Personal Assistant

Microsoft has unveiled Scout, an OpenClaw-inspired personal AI assistant designed to help users manage daily tasks, research, and digital workflows. Scout integrates with Microsoft 365, Edge, and Windows, positioning itself as a ubiquitous productivity companion.

Scout's architecture emphasizes proactive assistance — it can anticipate user needs based on calendar events, email patterns, and browsing behavior. The launch intensifies the competition between major tech companies for the personal AI assistant market, where early mover advantage could define user loyalty for years.
