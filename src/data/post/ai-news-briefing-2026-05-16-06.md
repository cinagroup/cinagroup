## Top 7 Stories

### 1. Anthropic Launches Cowork — Claude Desktop Agent That Works in Your Files

Anthropic announced **Cowork**, a new agentic feature for Claude Desktop that operates directly within users' file systems without requiring any coding. Cowork can read, organize, analyze, and modify files across documents, spreadsheets, and codebases. The agent understands file relationships and can execute multi-step workflows like "find all Q1 reports, summarize key metrics, and draft a presentation." Anthropic positioned this as a productivity tool for knowledge workers who spend hours on manual file management tasks. The feature represents a significant shift toward agentic AI that operates at the operating system level rather than just within browser windows.

### 2. Salesforce Rolls Out New Slackbot AI Agent for Workplace Automation

Salesforce has launched a **new Slackbot AI agent** designed to automate routine workplace tasks directly within Slack. The agent integrates with Salesforce's CRM ecosystem, enabling users to update records, generate reports, schedule meetings, and triage customer inquiries through natural language commands. This move intensifies the competition with Microsoft (Copilot) and Google (Duet AI) in the enterprise AI space. Salesforce emphasized that the agent operates with human oversight — critical actions like sending external communications require explicit approval.

### 3. OpenAI Launches ChatGPT for Personal Finance with Bank Account Integration

OpenAI announced that ChatGPT can now **connect to users' bank accounts** and provide personalized financial advice. The feature allows ChatGPT to analyze spending patterns, suggest budgets, identify subscription redundancies, and provide real-time financial insights. OpenAI stated that financial data is processed with end-to-end encryption and that the model does not retain sensitive account information between sessions. The launch positions ChatGPT as a personal financial assistant, competing with dedicated fintech apps. Privacy advocates have raised questions about the implications of sharing financial data with LLM providers.

### 4. Claude Code Pricing Sparks Debate — Goose Offers Free Alternative

Anthropic's **Claude Code** subscription, priced up to $200/month, has triggered a pricing debate in the AI coding space. In response, the open-source **Goose** project has gained attention for offering comparable agentic coding capabilities at no cost. Goose can write, debug, and review code, interact with terminals, and manage development workflows without subscription fees. The contrast highlights a growing tension in the AI developer tools market: premium closed-source agents versus community-driven open-source alternatives. Developers on social media have been debating whether the convenience and safety guarantees of Claude Code justify the premium price tag.

### 5. Listen Labs Raises $69M After Viral Billboard Hiring Stunt

**Listen Labs** has secured $69 million in funding following a viral hiring campaign that used billboards to recruit AI customer interview participants. The company specializes in AI-powered customer feedback and market research. The funding will be used to scale their AI interview platform, which conducts automated customer conversations, analyzes sentiment, and generates actionable insights for product teams. The billboard campaign itself became a case study in AI-native marketing, demonstrating how AI companies are using unconventional methods to reach both talent and customers.

### 6. Nous Research Releases NousCoder-14B — Open-Source Coding Model

**Nous Research** has released **NousCoder-14B**, an open-source coding model optimized for agentic development workflows. The model is designed to integrate with coding agents like Claude Code, Cursor, and open-source alternatives. NousCoder-14B supports multi-file editing, codebase understanding, and tool-calling capabilities. The release comes at a moment when the AI coding assistant market is rapidly expanding, and open-source models are closing the gap with proprietary offerings. The model is available under a permissive license, enabling commercial and research use.

### 7. Amazon Pressured Team to Develop AI Game — Project Trident

Reports indicate that **Amazon** pressured one of its game development teams to create an AI-driven game under the codename "Project Trident." Despite the push, the team was reportedly laid off afterward, raising questions about Amazon's AI gaming strategy and internal management practices. The story has sparked discussions about how tech giants are approaching AI in gaming — whether as a genuine creative tool or as a cost-cutting mechanism. Eurogamer's investigation revealed that the project struggled to find a clear product vision despite executive enthusiasm for AI integration.
