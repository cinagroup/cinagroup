## Top 7 Stories

1. **NVIDIA Customers Notified About AI-Related Price Hikes Above 15%**
   Bloomberg reported that NVIDIA has notified customers of price increases above 15% tied to AI demand, with Reuters and CNBC confirming the story. The hikes are attributed to sustained supply constraints on AI accelerators and surging datacenter buildout, signaling that GPU scarcity remains the dominant bottleneck in the AI buildout.

   For enterprises and cloud providers, this marks a notable shift from NVIDIA's historic per-generation price-performance improvements. Expect downstream pressure on cloud rental rates and AI inference costs in the coming quarters.

2. **Anthropic Could Aim to Raise $100 Billion in a Blockbuster IPO**
   The New York Times reports that Anthropic is exploring an IPO that could raise as much as $100 billion, a figure that would rank among the largest tech listings ever. CNBC adds that the filing is expected to list the AI backlash and safety scrutiny as prominent risk factors.

   A listing of this scale would validate the frontier-lab funding model and give public markets direct exposure to the Claude franchise. Watch for the S-1's safety disclosures — they could become the template for how AI companies talk to investors about risk.

3. **OpenAI Urges California to Strengthen Its AI Safety Bill**
   OpenAI has publicly backed strengthening California's AI safety legislation, per TechCrunch, arguing that well-calibrated rules can coexist with frontier model development. The stance echoes the company's own blog posts on zero data retention for frontier models and pacing model development in an era of cyber-critical capabilities.

   With the EU AI Act already in force and US federal regulation stalled, California remains the most likely venue for binding US AI law. OpenAI's endorsement — unusual for a frontier lab — could shift the political calculus for state lawmakers.

4. **Meta AI Launches a Mac App Aimed at Creators and Small Businesses**
   Meta rolled out a dedicated Mac app for Meta AI, positioning the assistant as a way to "talk to your apps" and streamline workflows for creators and small businesses (TechCrunch, The Verge, Axios). The desktop push brings Meta's assistant into direct competition with ChatGPT and Claude on the platform where many knowledge workers live.

   The move suggests Meta is serious about monetizing AI beyond ads, and it gives the company a beachhead in the productivity assistant market it has largely sat out so far.

5. **Ex-Google Engineer's Conviction for Stealing AI Secrets Partially Overturned**
   Reuters reports that a federal appeals court partially overturned the conviction of a former Google engineer found guilty of stealing AI trade secrets. The ruling narrows parts of the verdict while leaving other counts intact, extending a multi-year legal saga that has become a test case for protecting AI IP.

   The outcome matters beyond one case: courts are still defining what counts as a trade secret in the AI era, where model weights, training data, and research pipelines blur traditional IP boundaries.

6. **AI Agents Could Become Crypto's Next Billion Users**
   CoinDesk reports that AI agents are beginning to transact on-chain, paying with stablecoins, as autonomous agents handle everything from content deals to compute purchases. Agent-run wallets and machine-to-machine payments are emerging as a real use case for stablecoin rails.

   If agent economies take off, payment infrastructure designed for humans will need to adapt to machine-scale, high-frequency transactions — a shift with implications for fintech, gaming, and enterprise automation alike.

7. **Harvard Is Selling a $699 Course Taught by AI Clones of Its Faculty**
   The New York Times reports that Harvard is offering a $699 course taught by AI clones of its professors, one of the most high-profile experiments yet in AI-mediated higher education. The course raises questions about faculty consent, pedagogical quality, and the future of the university business model.

   If the experiment succeeds, expect other elite institutions to follow — and the debate over whether AI instructors expand access or commoditize teaching to intensify.
