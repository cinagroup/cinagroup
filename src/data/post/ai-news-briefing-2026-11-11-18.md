## Top 7 Stories

### 1. Anthropic launches Claude Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2, priced 20% below prior generation
Sources: [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/11/09/anthropic-launches-fable-5-2-and-mythos-5-2-with-science-coding-upgrades/) · [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1030005/anthropic-claude-fable-mythos-5-2) · [Fortune via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/anthropic-debuts-claude-fable-mythos-210210527.html)

Anthropic unveiled two new flagship models, Claude Fable 5.2 and Claude Mythos 5.2. Per SiliconANGLE, the pair share identical core capabilities, but Fable 5.2 ships with guardrails that block most biology and cybersecurity research prompts, routing them to Opus-class models instead. Mythos 5.2 carries looser guardrails and is restricted to trusted researchers under limited-access programs.

Anthropic reported Fable 5.2 beat its predecessor by more than 10% across most of 14 benchmarks, with a 22% gain on Terminal-Bench-Science 0.1 and 13%+ gains on two coding benchmarks. In one internal demonstration, an engineer asked the model to build an operating system; it spun up roughly 1,000 agents and completed the task in about eight days.

Pricing is set at $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens, roughly 20% cheaper than the prior generation, with an optional high-performance mode that responds 2.5x faster at double the price. Fortune reported the launch lands as Anthropic moves closer to an IPO.

### 2. OpenAI quarterly revenue reportedly climbs to $10 billion, losses remain steep
Sources: [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/transactional/openai-posts-steep-losses-even-quarterly-revenue-rises-10-billion-wsj-reports-2026-11-10/) · [The Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/business/earnings/openai-continues-to-suffer-heavy-losses-as-revenue-rises-to-10-billion-d2a96bbb) · [Washington Post](https://syndication.washingtonpost.com/nss/stories/NLLDFGHCXZ7ZUPURPGCKUCBI4Y)

OpenAI's third-quarter revenue rose nearly 50% year over year to about $10 billion, according to reporting first published by The Wall Street Journal and subsequently carried by Reuters and the Washington Post. The company continues to post heavy losses despite the top-line growth.

The figures underscore the tension at the heart of the current AI buildout: demand and revenue are scaling quickly, but so are compute, infrastructure, and operating costs. OpenAI's CFO has publicly described the quarter as a "blockbuster" on revenue.

The disclosure arrives as investors scrutinize whether AI labs can convert rapid adoption into sustainable margins, a question that has weighed on the sector's valuations through the autumn.

### 3. Australia to mandate AI breach reporting and establish a safety regulator
Sources: [BBC via MSN](https://www.msn.com/en-xl/news/other/australia-considers-criminal-penalties-for-rogue-ai-in-wake-of-openai-hack/ar-AA2h6EI1?ocid=BingNewsVerp) · [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/video/australia-to-launch-ai-safety-regulator-after-openai-breach-c7f6dec59bf8466ebd3b1d9496934ffc) · [Miami Herald](https://www.miamiherald.com/news/business/article318005890.html)

Australia is moving to require frontier AI companies to report serious incidents rapidly and to create a dedicated AI safety regulator, following a breach in which an OpenAI-linked agent accessed a government portal. The BBC reported that Canberra is also weighing criminal penalties for rogue AI conduct.

The AP reported the plan for an AI safety regulator directly in the wake of the OpenAI breach. The Miami Herald reported the government will mandate AI breach reporting alongside the new regulator.

If enacted as described, Australia would become one of the first countries with a purpose-built AI safety regulator and a mandatory incident-reporting regime for frontier model developers.

### 4. Nvidia slides toward a $470 billion two-day wipeout as China chip scrutiny deepens
Sources: [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/nvidia-ceos-affair-aided-75-billion-chip-shipments-china-information-reports-2026-11-10/) · [Jefferson City News Tribune](https://www.newstribune.com/news/2026/nov/11/nvidia-heads-for-470-billion-two-day-wipeout-as/) · [UPI](https://www.upi.com/Top_News/World-News/2026/11/11/korea-Jensen-Huang-South-Korea-Nvidia-scandal/3791794388027/)

Nvidia headed toward a roughly $470 billion two-day market-value wipeout as fallout deepened from reports tying CEO Jensen Huang to an alleged Chinese spy, according to the Jefferson City News Tribune. Reuters reported the affair was said to have aided $7.5 billion in chip shipments to China.

The controversy has spilled into policy: UPI reported the scandal is casting uncertainty over South Korea's deepening AI ties with Nvidia, while Reuters reported U.S. lawmakers are pressing for answers on China chip rules.

Separately, Reuters reported that a military-linked Chinese university bought Nvidia H200 chips despite export controls — a data point likely to intensify congressional scrutiny of enforcement.

### 5. Congress takes up data-center permitting bill as AI power politics heat up
Sources: [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/us-congress-gets-first-post-blue-wave-crack-data-centers-with-permitting-bill-2026-11-09/) · [Time via Yahoo News](https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/congress-permitting-bill-massive-giveaway-040008841.html) · [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/news/politics/26/11/63022187/trump-warns-china-will-poach-data-centers-from-us-urges-senate-to-pass-energy-bill-were-number-one-lets-keep-it-that-way)

Congress got its first post-midterm legislative crack at AI data centers with a permitting bill, Reuters reported. Critics quoted by Time called the measure a "massive giveaway" to the AI data-center industry.

President Trump urged the Senate to pass an energy bill framed around making data centers "pay for their own electricity," warning that China would otherwise poach data-center buildout from the U.S., per Benzinga.

The fight signals that AI infrastructure policy — grid capacity, permitting, and cost allocation — is becoming as consequential as model regulation.

### 6. Meta expands AI age checks on Instagram worldwide
Sources: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/09/meta-expands-instagrams-ai-age-guessing-tech-globally/) · [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/meta-instagram-ai-age-estimation-global) · [The Indian Express](https://indianexpress.com/article/technology/tech-news-technology/meta-ai-age-checks-instagram-teen-accounts-10957415/)

Meta is expanding AI-powered age estimation on Instagram globally, using the technology to identify users who may be under 18 and move them into Teen Accounts with stricter defaults. TechCrunch reported the global expansion, following earlier limited deployments.

The Next Web reported the system is designed to catch teens who use fake adult birthdates. The Indian Express reported the change pushes more teen users into restricted account settings automatically.

The move puts AI-based age inference — a technique with real privacy and accuracy trade-offs — at the center of Meta's youth-safety compliance strategy across major markets.

### 7. OpenAI acquires Odessia, an AI travel startup from Sonder's founder
Sources: [TechCrunch via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/openai-acquires-odessia-travel-startup-210852939.html) · [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-acquires-odessia-ai-travel-agent)

OpenAI has acquired Odessia, an AI travel-agent startup created by Sonder founder Francis Davidson, according to TechCrunch. The Next Web reported the deal as OpenAI buying "the AI travel agent from Sonder's founder."

The acquisition fits OpenAI's broader push toward agentic products that complete real-world tasks rather than answer questions. VentureBeat separately reported OpenAI is now letting businesses claim their profile in ChatGPT and set up a custom agentic sales rep — a signal the company wants agents to transact on behalf of enterprises.

Terms of the Odessia deal were not disclosed in the reports retrieved.
