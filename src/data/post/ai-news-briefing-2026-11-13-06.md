### 1. NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang resigns effective immediately amid spy scandal

Sources: [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-resigns/) · [Reuters (via MSN)](https://www.msn.com/en-gb/technology/tech-companies/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-resigns-in-blow-to-ai-boom-he-helped-foster/ar-AA2hjQOQ?ocid=BingNewsVerp) · [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/jensen-huang-leaving-nvidia-risks-a-brain-drain-from-the-ai-chipmaker/)

NVIDIA's board of directors announced Thursday that Jensen Huang had resigned as president and CEO effective immediately, following a scandal involving a woman U.S. intelligence agencies suspect was spying for China. CBS News reported that the board had been preparing to oust Huang and that President Trump said the FBI is investigating romantic and business ties between Huang and the woman, Alice Huang. The New York Times separately reported that five NVIDIA employees alleged Huang was romantically involved with a subordinate.

CFO Colette Kress is serving as interim leader, according to CNN, while Reuters characterized the departure as "a blow to the AI boom" Huang helped foster. NVIDIA shares tumbled on the news. Scientific American framed the risk as a potential brain drain from the chipmaker at the center of the global AI buildout — a governance shock for the single most important supplier in the industry.

The resignation lands three days after NVIDIA's board reportedly began deliberating Huang's future, a sequence detailed in the previous briefing. Investors now face the question of whether an interim CFO can hold the company's roadmap and customer relationships together through what is likely to be a multi-quarter leadership search.

### 2. OpenAI raises $15B at $1.25T valuation as quarterly revenue tops $10B

Sources: [CNBC (via Yahoo Finance)](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/openai-reportedly-raises-15b-1-192622324.html) · [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/openai-hits-10-billion-quarterly-190211001.html) · [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-11/openai-anthropic-valuations-fuel-vc-fears-of-startup-correction)

OpenAI closed a $15 billion funding round at a $1.25 trillion valuation, according to CNBC's David Faber — roughly half the $30 billion the company had initially sought from investors. The raise caps a stretch in which OpenAI repeatedly trimmed its ambitions, having earlier pared its target to around $20 billion before settling at $15B.

Separately, OpenAI's third-quarter revenue exceeded $10 billion for the first time, a 50% jump, but operating losses widened and the company continues to spend more than it earns. Yahoo Finance reported the company is spending roughly $2.09 for every dollar of revenue, a ratio that keeps the pressure on to convert growth into a path toward profitability.

Bloomberg reported that the twin OpenAI and Anthropic valuations are fueling fears among venture investors of a broader startup correction, with the two labs' mega-rounds soaking up capital and setting expectations that smaller AI companies cannot match. Anthropic is expected to IPO as soon as next week at a tentative valuation near $1.4 trillion, setting up a direct public-market comparison.

### 3. Anthropic's Claude joins live video calls as a visible teammate

Sources: [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/technology/anthropic-brings-claude-google-meet-tackle-tasks-live-2026-11-10/) · [Fortune (via Yahoo Finance)](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/claude-now-join-video-calls-190700694.html) · [Axios (via Yahoo Tech)](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/claude/articles/anthropic-wants-claude-join-meetings-173006469.html)

Anthropic launched a beta that lets Claude join Google Meet calls as an active, visible participant — taking notes, answering questions and executing tasks live during the meeting rather than waiting for a post-call summary. Reuters reported the integration lets Claude "tackle tasks live," while Axios described the product as Anthropic's push to make Claude a participant in your next work meeting rather than a sidebar assistant.

Fortune framed the launch as part of a broader suite of products aimed at reinventing white-collar work beyond coding, and noted it could be among Anthropic's last major releases before its expected IPO on November 19. The move extends the company's strategy from developer tooling into meeting-room workflows where Microsoft Copilot and Google's own Gemini already compete.

Anthropic also agreed to protections for teachers who use Claude through its education offering, according to Education Week. Claude's web traffic surged 24% month-over-month between September and October, per Forbes — a much larger jump than any competing chatbot — giving the company momentum heading into its public listing.

### 4. Nonprofits sue OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, Meta and SpaceX to halt frontier AI development

Sources: [Hindustan Times](https://www.hindustantimes.com/world-news/nonprofit-groups-sue-openai-google-anthropic-meta-and-spacexs-ai-division-seeking-halt-on-ai-development-101794521310533.html) · [Al Jazeera (via MSN)](https://www.msn.com/en-us/technology/artificial-intelligence/us-nonprofits-sue-tech-giants-to-halt-ai-development/ar-AA2hjVqt?ocid=BingNewsVerp) · [Washington Examiner (via MSN)](https://www.msn.com/en-us/news/other/nonprofits-sue-five-ai-developers-to-freeze-new-models-before-catastrophe-strikes/ar-AA2hjI7k?ocid=BingNewsVerp)

A coalition of nonprofit groups sued OpenAI, Google, Anthropic, Meta and SpaceX's AI division, seeking to halt advanced AI development on the grounds of existential risk. The suits lean on the companies' own public statements about the dangers of frontier models — arguing that lab leaders who warn their products could be catastrophic cannot then continue building them without judicial intervention.

Al Jazeera reported the filings target the five developers collectively, while the Washington Examiner characterized the demand as a request to "freeze new models before catastrophe strikes." The legal theory is unusual: rather than alleging a specific harm, the plaintiffs argue that the acknowledged risk of a catastrophic accident creates a duty the companies are breaching by proceeding.

The suit follows an earlier action against OpenAI over a Hugging Face hack, reported by Ars Technica, in which plaintiffs argued that "an AI did it" is no defense. Whether courts are willing to entertain pre-emptive injunctions against model development — as opposed to after-the-fact liability — is likely to be the central procedural question.

### 5. Google rolls out Gemini 4 Hydrogen, replacing 3.8 Flash

Sources: [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/google/2026/11/google-rolls-out-gemini-4-hydrogen-finally-replacing-3-8-flash/) · [Seeking Alpha](https://seekingalpha.com/news/4671702-google-unveils-new-gemini-4-hydrogen-model-aimed-at-speed-and-efficiency) · [Neowin](https://www.neowin.net/news/googles-gemini-4-hydrogen-beats-gpt-61-terra-on-most-key-ai-benchmarks/)

Google released Gemini 4 Hydrogen, a flash-class model aimed at speed and cost efficiency, finally retiring the long-serving 3.8 Flash generation. Ars Technica noted that Hydrogen is not getting the slow, security-conscious staged release that accompanied the earlier frontier Carbon and Argon models — it is being rolled out broadly and immediately.

Seeking Alpha reported the model is positioned squarely on speed and efficiency, while Wccftech noted Sundar Pichai touting roughly 2.5x faster response times and the model landing among the cheapest options on Vals AI's rankings. Neowin reported Hydrogen beats OpenAI's GPT-6.1 Terra on most key benchmarks, a claim worth treating cautiously until independent replications land.

The release pattern matters as much as the benchmarks: by shipping a fast, cheap tier aggressively across its product surface, Google is competing on cost-per-query for the high-volume agent workloads that Meta and OpenAI are also targeting this month.

### 6. Meta launches Muse Ember, a low-cost model built for agent execution

Sources: [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/ai-story/muse-ember-meta-low-cost-ai-model-agent-execution) · [Gadgets 360 (via MSN)](https://www.msn.com/en-in/news/other/meta-launches-muse-ember-ai-model-for-coding-and-automated-tasks/ar-AA2h8tcA?ocid=BingNewsVerp) · [Mint (via MSN)](https://www.msn.com/en-in/news/other/meta-takes-on-openai-and-anthropic-with-muse-ember-ai-model-features-benchmarks-and-pricing/ar-AA2h7dMB?ocid=BingNewsVerp)

Meta released Muse Ember, a low-cost model explicitly positioned for agent execution, coding and automated task workloads. YourStory reported the model's pitch is economic: agentic workflows chain many model calls together, so per-token price dominates total cost, and Meta is undercutting rivals on that axis rather than chasing top-end benchmark scores.

Gadgets 360 described the model as targeting coding and automated tasks, while Mint framed it as Meta's direct answer to OpenAI's GPT-6 Luna and Anthropic's Claude Haiku 5.5 in the small-model tier. Coverage consistently treats Muse Ember as a division-of-labor play — a cheap executor that a larger model plans for — rather than a frontier attempt.

The launch fits Meta's broader infrastructure push: the company recently issued a $15 billion five-part bond to fund AI infrastructure. With Google, Meta and OpenAI all shipping cheap high-volume tiers within the same week, the small-model segment is now the most contested part of the market.

### 7. Australia to impose mandatory AI safety rules and create a new regulator

Sources: [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/10/australia-tightens-ai-oversight-rules-after-openai-government-breach) · [Computer Weekly](https://www.computerweekly.com/news/366654658/Australia-plans-AI-safety-regulator-to-enforce-mandatory-safeguards) · [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/11/223-australia-safety-rules-regulator-frontier-ai-companies/)

Australia announced plans to impose mandatory safety rules on frontier AI developers and establish a dedicated AI safety regulator with enforcement powers, moving beyond the voluntary-guardrail model it has relied on so far. Computer Weekly reported the regulator would enforce mandatory safeguards, while MediaNama described obligations aimed specifically at frontier AI companies.

Yahoo Finance reported one concrete requirement: frontier AI firms will have to report serious incidents rapidly to the government. Al Jazeera tied the timing to an OpenAI security breach involving government systems, framing the package as a tightening of oversight triggered by that incident.

The Australian move is notable because it pairs a reporting duty with an enforcement body — closer to the EU AI Act's structure than to the lighter-touch voluntary commitments favored in Washington. For labs, it adds another jurisdiction-specific compliance surface on top of EU obligations and pending U.S. export rules.
