## Top 7 Stories

### 1. US Judge Blocks Pentagon's Blacklisting of Anthropic

A US federal judge has blocked the Pentagon's blacklisting of Anthropic, ruling that the Trump administration's attempts to punish and ban the AI lab were unlawful. Reuters, NPR, and The Guardian all reported on the ruling, with the judge describing the measures against Anthropic as "illegal and baseless."

The decision is a landmark check on executive power over AI companies and removes a major threat to Anthropic's ability to work with the US defense establishment. It also sets a legal precedent for how far the government can go in restricting a frontier lab, and it injects fresh uncertainty into Washington's broader AI-policy fights.

### 2. Anthropic: Automated Researchers Can Reliably Mitigate Alignment Failures

Anthropic published research showing that automated researchers — AI systems that run safety experiments on other models — can reliably mitigate alignment failures. TechCrunch described the work as a rare peek at self-improving AI, noting that Anthropic researchers demonstrated systems capable of finding and fixing alignment problems at scale.

If automated safety research scales, it could shift the economics of AI safety from scarce human expertise to machine-speed auditing of frontier models. The work also raises the stakes of the debate over self-improving AI: the same techniques that fix alignment failures could, in principle, accelerate capabilities.

### 3. Andreessen Horowitz Launches 'Machine Age' Fund to Tackle AI Supply Bottlenecks

Andreessen Horowitz is launching a "Machine Age" fund aimed at AI supply bottlenecks, according to an exclusive WSJ report. The fund is designed to back companies addressing the physical constraints — power, data centers, chips, and manufacturing — that now limit AI buildout more than model quality does.

The launch underscores how AI investing is rotating from pure software toward infrastructure. With hyperscalers locking up GPU capacity and power becoming the industry's binding constraint, investors are betting that the winners of the AI era will include the suppliers of its physical foundations, not just the model labs.

### 4. Marvell's Google AI Deal Could Generate Up to $120 Billion — Stock Fell 8%

Marvell announced a custom AI chip deal with Google that could be worth up to $120 billion over its lifetime, yet the stock fell 8% as investors sought clarity on when the revenue would actually materialize, Reuters and Yahoo Finance reported. The selloff deepened as analysts questioned the timing and payoff structure of the agreement.

The deal is another sign that Google is aggressively building custom silicon to reduce dependence on NVIDIA, while Marvell cements itself as a top custom-chip partner. But the market's skeptical reaction shows that even historic AI contracts are being priced against delivery timelines — a sign of cooling patience with AI-era promises.

### 5. X Reveals Foreign Adversary Behind Fake Accounts Pushing Anti-Data-Center Messaging

X said it uncovered a coordinated network of fake accounts backed by a foreign adversary that was disseminating anti-data-center messaging, Fox Business reported. The disclosure adds a geopolitical dimension to the growing local backlash against AI data centers, which face opposition over power use, water, and noise across the US.

The finding suggests that some of the grassroots resistance to data center buildout may be amplified by state actors seeking to slow American AI infrastructure. Expect governments and utilities to scrutinize astroturfing campaigns as data center siting battles intensify.

### 6. California Lawmakers Crack Down on AI Used in Public Comment

California lawmakers are moving to crack down on the use of AI to generate public comments in legislative and regulatory processes, the Los Angeles Times reported. The measures respond to concerns that mass AI-generated submissions are flooding public-comment systems and drowning out genuine citizen voices.

California's move could set the template for how states regulate AI in democratic participation. As AI-generated lobbying scales, expect more jurisdictions to require disclosure of AI-written input — and new fights over how to verify human authorship in public discourse.

### 7. South Korea's 'AI for All' Push Gives Free Access to Every Citizen

South Korea is rolling out an "AI for All" initiative that gives every citizen free access to AI services, according to the WSJ. The program is among the most ambitious national efforts to make AI a public utility, positioning AI access alongside broadband as basic infrastructure.

The move is a striking contrast to the US and EU, where AI policy is dominated by regulation debates. If South Korea's experiment works, it could accelerate the global race to universal AI access — and put pressure on other governments to treat AI capability as a public good rather than a premium product.
