## Top 7 Stories

### 1. OpenAI, Google and Anthropic discussing collaboration on AI safety

The three leading frontier labs have reportedly been in discussions about coordinating on AI safety issues, per CNBC. The talks signal that even fierce commercial rivals see shared risk-management infrastructure — from evaluations to deployment guardrails — as something that cannot be solved by any single company.

The reported discussions come as regulators and researchers alike push for cross-lab standards, and as internal pressure within the labs grows. The story is notable less for any concrete agreement than for the direction of travel: the industry's biggest players exploring common ground on safety rather than competing on it.

Source: [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/15/open-ai-google-anthropic-safety.html)

### 2. Microsoft unveils new Surface laptops with Nvidia chips

Microsoft launched a new generation of Surface laptops built around Nvidia AI silicon, headlined by the Surface Laptop Ultra at $2,599. Reporting describes the machines as capable of running AI agents locally — without round-tripping to the cloud — a meaningful shift for privacy, latency, and offline capability.

The launch is a landmark for on-device AI: it moves "AI PC" from marketing label to a machine that can execute agent workloads at the edge. It also deepens the Microsoft-Nvidia hardware partnership at a moment when Nvidia's chips are the scarce resource underpinning the entire AI build-out.

Sources: [Seeking Alpha](https://seekingalpha.com/news/4651125-microsoft-unveils-new-surface-laptops-with-nvidia-chips), [Benzinga on MSN](https://www.msn.com/en-us/news/other/nvidia-inside-microsofts-new-laptop-runs-ai-agents-without-the-cloud/ar-AA2dKEuk?ocid=BingNewsVerp)

### 3. Trump signs order renaming AI 'super intelligence'; says dissenters are 'THE ENEMY'

A week after signing an executive order renaming artificial intelligence as "super intelligence," President Trump said anyone who does not use the new term is "THE ENEMY," according to Politico. The episode injects an unusual linguistic directive into federal AI discourse.

It matters because naming shapes policy: what the government calls the technology affects how agencies scope contracts, risk frameworks, and oversight. The remarks also illustrate how polarized the AI policy environment has become heading into 2027.

Source: [Politico on MSN](https://www.msn.com/en-us/news/other/trump-says-anyone-who-does-not-refer-to-ai-as-super-intelligence-is-the-enemy/ar-AA2dQO9A)

### 4. AP-NORC poll: most Americans think AI is developing too fast

A new AP-NORC poll found that the vast majority of Americans believe artificial intelligence is developing too quickly, and that concern crosses party lines. Worry about AI is one of the rare issues with bipartisan agreement among the public.

The finding is a caution flag for companies and policymakers betting on rapid, lightly-regulated deployment. If public anxiety keeps climbing, expect it to feed into state-level regulation, election-year politics, and product trust narratives.

Source: [AP via CNYHomepage](https://www.cnyhomepage.com/news/national-news/ap-most-americans-think-artificial-intelligence-is-developing-too-fast-a-new-ap-norc-poll-finds/)

### 5. GSA issues AI acquisition policy for new federal contracts

The US General Services Administration issued a new AI acquisition policy governing how agencies buy AI for new contracts, per FedScoop. The policy sets expectations for how the federal government procures and vets AI tools.

Because the federal government is among the largest buyers of technology in the world, GSA procurement rules become de facto product requirements for vendors. The policy is an early look at how "AI governance" turns into concrete contractual language.

Source: [FedScoop](https://fedscoop.com/gsa-issued-an-ai-acquisition-policy-for-new-contracts-heres-what-it-says/)

### 6. RSA launches Agent ID to secure and govern enterprise AI agents

RSA introduced Agent ID, a product aimed at identifying, securing, and governing the AI agents enterprises are now deploying. It lands amid a broader scramble to build identity and access control for non-human actors that act autonomously inside corporate systems.

The move reflects a maturing market: as AI agents gain the ability to take actions — not just answer questions — the question of "who is this agent, and what is it allowed to do?" becomes a security problem. Agent identity is emerging as a distinct category alongside human IAM.

Source: [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/rsa-launches-agent-id-to-secure-and-govern-enterprise-ai-agents/)

Reporting indicates OpenAI's annualized revenue run rate is approaching $50 billion, yet lands roughly $20 billion below what the company had previously signaled to investors. The gap highlights the tension between AI's enormous capital needs and the pace of actual monetization.

The numbers matter for the whole ecosystem: OpenAI's revenue trajectory is a proxy for whether the industry's trillion-dollar infrastructure bets can be serviced. Watch for follow-on scrutiny of the 2027 cash-flow outlook flagged by analysts.
