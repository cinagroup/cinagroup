### 1. Nvidia board reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO and chair after political pressure

Nvidia's board voted unanimously to reinstate Jensen Huang as both CEO and board chair, reversing a leadership change that had unsettled the chipmaker's investors. Reporting indicates the decision followed direct pressure from the Trump administration and lobbying from Silicon Valley allies, who argued that removing Huang mid-cycle would destabilize the company's AI accelerator roadmap.

Huang reportedly ended the day roughly $34 billion richer on paper as the stock rallied on the news. The episode has become a case study in how political and industrial policy concerns now intersect with corporate governance at the most systemically important AI hardware firm.

### 2. Anthropic launches Claude Ship for autonomous code review and approval
Sources: [Mint (MSN)](https://www.msn.com/en-in/news/other/anthropic-launches-claude-ship-to-help-ai-agents-review-fix-and-approve-code-changes/ar-AA2i5ckb) · [Tom's Guide (MSN)](https://www.msn.com/en-us/technology/software/claude-can-now-write-review-and-approve-its-own-code-here-s-why-that-s-a-big-deal/ar-AA2i6zkU) · [Miami Herald](https://www.miamiherald.com/news/business/article318219375.html)

Anthropic released Claude Ship, a feature that lets Claude agents review, fix, and approve code changes — including pull requests — without waiting for a human reviewer. The tool is aimed at teams that want to compress the code review bottleneck while keeping changes inside an auditable agent workflow.

The launch marks another step toward Anthropic positioning Claude as an end-to-end software engineering agent rather than just an assistant. Commentators flagged the governance questions raised by letting a model approve its own code, an issue likely to intensify as agentic developer tooling matures.

### 3. Amazon: hackers' AI agents found and exploited a zero-day with minimal human input
Sources: [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/11/amazon-says-hackers-ai-agents-found-and-exploited-0-day-with-little-human-input/) · [Dark Reading](https://www.darkreading.com/cyberattacks-data-breaches/ai-agents-flaw-discovery-root-access-11-minutes) · [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/security/amazon-says-attackers-ai-agents-found-and-exploited-a-zero-day-in-11-minutes-but-no-patched-release-is-available)

Amazon's security team documented an intrusion in which attackers' AI agents discovered and exploited a zero-day vulnerability in MONAI Label — reaching root access in roughly 11 minutes with little human involvement. The rapid autonomous exploit chain is among the clearest public examples yet of offensive AI agents operating at machine speed.

CISA subsequently told U.S. agencies to patch the flaw, even though a fully patched upstream release was not immediately available. The incident has renewed debate over whether defensive automation can keep pace with offense when both sides run agents.

### 4. OpenAI merges Chat and Work in ChatGPT, unifying usage limits
Sources: [TechCrunch (MSN)](https://www.msn.com/en-us/news/other/openai-says-heavy-chatgpt-users-may-hit-limits-sooner-as-it-merges-chat-and-work/ar-AA2iea3j) · [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/383963/openai-changes-chatgpt-drastically-cut-usage-limits) · [Moneycontrol](https://www.moneycontrol.com/technology/openai-merges-chat-and-work-in-chatgpt-for-pro-users-shared-usage-meter-goes-live-article-14090038.html)

OpenAI began merging its Chat and Work experiences inside ChatGPT, moving paid tiers onto a single shared usage allowance. The upshot is that heavy users — particularly those leaning on Codex — may hit their limits sooner than before, since consumption across both surfaces now draws from the same meter.

The change is being rolled out across Plus, Pro, and Business plans, with reporting suggesting combined Codex usage limits take effect from December. Power users have pushed back, arguing the consolidation effectively reduces usable capacity on existing subscriptions.

### 5. Tidal to ban fully AI-generated music and delete millions of tracks
Sources: [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/11/tidal-will-ban-fully-ai-generated-music-on-december-9/) · [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/gabrielalinzainescu/2026/11/25/tidal-will-delete-27-million-ai-generated-tracks-starting-december-9/) · [Neowin](https://www.neowin.net/news/tidal-bans-ai-generated-music-from-its-platform/)

Tidal announced it will ban wholly AI-generated music starting December 9, becoming the first major streaming platform to take that stance. Around 27 million tracks are expected to be removed, a striking share of the catalog that underscores how much synthetic content has already flooded streaming services.

The policy targets fully synthetic tracks rather than AI-assisted production, but enforcement at scale remains the open question. Rights holders and human artists have broadly welcomed the move as a signal on attribution and royalties.
