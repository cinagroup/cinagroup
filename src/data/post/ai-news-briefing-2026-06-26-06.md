## Top 7 Stories

### 1. OpenAI Delays IPO to 2027

OpenAI is leaning toward postponing its highly anticipated IPO until next year, according to The New York Times. The decision comes amid market uncertainties and unmet revenue targets, with the company reportedly spooked by SpaceX's recent public offering challenges.

The delay signals caution in the AI sector as valuations face scrutiny. OpenAI had been targeting a 2026 public debut, but shifting market conditions and investor skepticism about AI monetization timelines have prompted a more conservative approach.

### 2. OpenAI and Broadcom Unveil Jalapeño Custom AI Chip

OpenAI has partnered with Broadcom to launch its first custom inference chip, codenamed Jalapeño. The chip is specifically optimized for large language model inference workloads, marking OpenAI's push to control its full technology stack.

The Jalapeño chip aims to reduce reliance on NVIDIA GPUs and improve ChatGPT's performance and cost efficiency. This vertical integration strategy mirrors moves by Google and Amazon to develop custom silicon for their AI services.

### 3. Anthropic Accuses Alibaba of Massive AI Distillation Attack

Anthropic has filed complaints alleging that Alibaba conducted a systematic campaign to illicitly extract capabilities from Claude AI models. The company claims Alibaba used 25,000 fake accounts to conduct 28.8 million API exchanges between April and June 2026.

The accusations have sent Alibaba's stock sliding 33% and prompted Anthropic to urge Congress to crack down on AI distillation by Chinese rivals. This incident highlights growing tensions over intellectual property in the AI race and could lead to stricter export controls.

### 4. Google Delays Gemini 3.5 Pro Launch to July

Google has pushed back the release of Gemini 3.5 Pro, its new frontier AI model, as the company works to catch up with Anthropic and OpenAI. The delay comes as Alphabet stock feels pressure from reports that five top Google AI researchers have departed in just seven days.

The setback underscores Google's struggle to maintain its AI leadership position. The company is also revamping its AI coding strike team and facing increased competition as Microsoft and others gain ground in the enterprise AI market.

### 5. California Launches First State AI Workforce Monitoring Tool

California has become the first U.S. state to launch a tool for monitoring and tracking artificial intelligence's impact on the workforce. The initiative aims to provide data-driven insights into how AI adoption affects employment, wages, and job displacement across industries.

The move positions California as a leader in AI policy and could serve as a model for other states and federal legislation. As AI agents increasingly automate tasks, policymakers are seeking better data to guide labor market interventions.

### 6. NVIDIA Announces Halos for Robotics Safety System

NVIDIA has unveiled Halos for Robotics, described as the industry's first full-stack safety system for physical AI. The platform is designed to advance robotics safety in industrial and commercial applications, addressing growing concerns about autonomous systems operating alongside humans.

The announcement comes as NVIDIA pushes robot safety into factories through partnerships with companies like Agility Robotics. Halos represents NVIDIA's expansion beyond AI chips into comprehensive robotics platforms, competing with specialized robotics firms.

### 7. Meta Launches Business AI Agent for Enterprise

Meta has introduced a new business AI agent designed to expand its enterprise services globally. The agent aims to help businesses automate customer service, sales, and operational workflows using Meta's AI infrastructure.

The launch marks Meta's push into the enterprise AI market, competing with Microsoft, Google, and Salesforce. As AI agents become central to business automation, Meta is leveraging its AI research to capture enterprise customers beyond its social media and advertising core.
