## Top 7 Stories

### 1. US AI Safety Agency Head Resigns After Just 3 Months

The head of the US Commerce Department's AI safety arm has resigned, multiple outlets reported Monday, dealing a significant blow to the Trump administration's AI governance efforts. The official, who served for only three months, departed amid what Axios described as a "secret Trump administration battle to fight Chinese AI." The resignation raises urgent questions about the future direction of federal AI safety policy at a moment when frontier models are growing more powerful and calls for international oversight are intensifying.

The departure comes just days after Google DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis publicly urged Washington to spearhead a global AI standards body. With the US AI Safety Institute now lacking its top leader, the path forward for domestic AI regulation — and America's role in shaping global norms — looks increasingly uncertain.

### 2. Meta and Anthropic in Talks for Landmark $10 Billion AI Compute Deal

Meta is exploring a deal to lease massive amounts of AI computing infrastructure to Anthropic in an agreement that could be worth up to $10 billion, according to reports from The New York Times, CNBC, and CNN. The deal would see Anthropic — maker of the Claude family of models — tap into Meta's enormous AI infrastructure buildout, giving it the compute scale needed to compete with OpenAI and Google at the frontier.

The talks signal a pragmatic shift in the AI industry: rather than every major player building entirely independent infrastructure stacks, strategic partnerships around compute are becoming the norm. If finalized, the deal would be one of the largest AI infrastructure agreements ever struck, and could reshape the competitive dynamics between the leading frontier labs.

### 3. Google Developing 'Frozen' Chip for Radically More Efficient AI Inference

Alphabet's Google is developing a new custom chip — codenamed "Frozen" — designed to run its AI models far more efficiently than current hardware, The Information reported Monday. The news sent Alphabet shares higher, with investors betting that a breakthrough in inference efficiency could dramatically lower the cost of serving Google's Gemini models and other AI workloads.

The "Frozen" chip reportedly focuses on inference optimization rather than training, targeting the growing share of AI compute spend that goes to serving models in production. If successful, it would strengthen Google's hand against Nvidia's dominance in the inference market and follow the company's TPU strategy of building specialized silicon for every stage of the AI pipeline.

### 4. AMD Launches Helios Rack System, Adding Microsoft as Newest Buyer

AMD unveiled Helios, its first rack-scale AI system designed to compete directly with Nvidia's DGX and HGX platforms, and announced Microsoft as its newest major customer. The move, reported Monday by CNBC, marks AMD's most aggressive push yet into the data-center AI market, where Nvidia has held a commanding lead.

The Helios system integrates AMD's Instinct GPUs into a full rack solution, simplifying deployment for cloud providers and enterprises. Microsoft's commitment — adding to its existing Azure AI infrastructure — signals that hyperscalers are serious about diversifying their AI silicon supply chains beyond Nvidia, a theme reinforced by the same reports showing Nvidia briefly losing its crown as the world's most valuable company.

### 5. DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis Calls for US-Led Global AI Watchdog

Google DeepMind chief Demis Hassabis has called on Washington to establish and lead an international AI standards body, warning that frontier AI models are raising urgent security concerns that no single nation can address alone. In interviews with the Los Angeles Times and CNBC, Hassabis argued that the US must take the lead in creating a "CERN for AI safety" — an international institution with the authority to set and enforce safety standards for advanced AI development.

The call comes as DeepMind expands its own biosecurity efforts, announcing a new bioresilience initiative aimed at preventing misuse of AI in biological threats. Hassabis's intervention adds heavyweight industry pressure to the global AI governance conversation at a moment when the US safety infrastructure is facing leadership turmoil.

### 6. Inference Startup Infinity Raises $15M from OpenAI and Anthropic Researchers

Infinity, a startup focused on AI inference infrastructure, has raised $15 million in a funding round backed by researchers from OpenAI and Anthropic, TechCrunch reported Monday. The round, led by Touring Capital, underscores the growing investor appetite for companies tackling the inference layer of the AI stack — the part of the pipeline where models actually serve predictions to users.

The involvement of researchers from two of the top three frontier labs is notable: it suggests that even the scientists building the most advanced models see inference efficiency as a critical bottleneck and investment opportunity. Infinity joins a wave of inference-focused startups attracting capital as the industry shifts from a training-dominated GPU market to one where inference workloads drive the majority of compute demand.

### 7. Roblox Announces 'Build' — AI Tools That Let Anyone Create Games

Roblox unveiled "Build," a suite of AI-powered creation tools that allow users to generate games and experiences using natural language prompts, Mashable reported Monday. The tools represent a major step toward Roblox's vision of making game development accessible to its massive user base — over 70 million daily active users — regardless of coding ability.

Build leverages generative AI to create 3D environments, script game logic, and design interactive elements from simple text descriptions. The announcement places Roblox at the forefront of the AI-native user-generated content trend, alongside similar moves from platforms like YouTube and TikTok. If successful, it could dramatically expand the Roblox creator economy while raising new questions about content moderation at AI scale.

## What to Watch

**AI Governance in Flux.** With the US AI safety chief's resignation and Hassabis's call for international standards, the next 30 days could determine the trajectory of AI regulation for years. Watch for whether the White House appoints a replacement quickly — or whether the vacancy signals a broader deprioritization of AI safety at the federal level.

**Meta-Anthropic Deal Finalization.** If the $10 billion compute deal closes, it would be the largest AI infrastructure partnership in history. The terms — pricing, duration, chip allocation — will reveal how the industry is valuing AI compute and whether Meta's infrastructure bet is paying off beyond its own AI efforts.

**Inference Wars Heat Up.** Between Google's "Frozen" chip, AMD's Helios rack, and Nvidia defending its turf, the AI inference hardware market is entering a period of intense competition. Lower inference costs directly translate to cheaper AI services — good news for consumers and enterprises alike.

**AI Creation Tools Go Mainstream.** Roblox Build is the latest example of generative AI moving from text and images into interactive, 3D experiences. Expect every major platform with a creator ecosystem to follow suit, accelerating the shift toward AI-native content generation at massive scale.
