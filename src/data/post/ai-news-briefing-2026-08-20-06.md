## Top 7 Stories

### 1. OpenAI introduces Zero Data Retention for frontier models
OpenAI announced Zero Data Retention for its frontier models, a new customer privacy option that promises prompts and outputs are not stored after processing. TechCrunch reports the move is widely read as an attempt to one-up Anthropic, whose enterprise offerings have long marketed stronger data-handling commitments to security-conscious customers.

For enterprises weighing which lab to trust with sensitive workloads, data-handling policy is now a first-order buying criterion. If OpenAI matches or beats Anthropic on privacy terms, the enterprise differentiation battle shifts elsewhere — likely to price, agent tooling, and model quality.

### 2. Google packs Search and Gemini with new AI study tools
Google launched a suite of AI study features spanning Search and Gemini, including step-by-step homework help, personalized study plans, and interactive practice. The Verge adds that Gemini is getting a dedicated student hub designed to organize courses, materials, and AI tutoring sessions in one place.

This is Google's most direct answer yet to ChatGPT for Teens, which OpenAI rolled out days ago. Education is becoming a major consumer AI battleground, and Google's distribution advantage — Search plus Android — gives it a real shot at owning the classroom assistant slot.

### 3. Researchers say OpenAI revoked their access to a limited cyber program
TechCrunch reports that a group of security researchers says OpenAI revoked their access to its limited cyber program — a restricted initiative that gives vetted researchers controlled access to models for defensive security research. The researchers claim the revocation came without clear explanation, raising questions about how OpenAI governs transparency in sensitive domains.

The episode follows OpenAI's own policy push for "pacing" cyber-critical model development and tighter democratic oversight in national security. Expect continued scrutiny over how frontier labs balance openness with safety gating — and who gets to decide where the line sits.

### 4. Meta AI is getting a Mac app
Meta is bringing its AI assistant to macOS with a dedicated Meta AI app, marking the assistant's first native desktop presence beyond Meta's social apps. The app is expected to bundle chat, image generation, and agentic tools into a standalone experience aimed at desktop productivity users.

For Meta, a native Mac app is a distribution play: it puts Meta AI in front of users outside Facebook, Instagram, and WhatsApp. It also signals that the assistant war is moving to the desktop, where OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google are all competing for the same real estate.

### 5. Cognition's CEO denies report that SpaceX tried to acquire the startup
Cognition CEO Scott Wu denied a report that SpaceX attempted to acquire the AI coding startup, calling the claim inaccurate. The denial follows weeks of speculation about the future of Devin, Cognition's autonomous software-engineering agent, and the startup's independence after its eye-catching valuation run.

Even if the acquisition report was wrong, the rumor itself underscores how hot AI-agent startups have become — and how aggressively strategic buyers are circling. Devin's trajectory remains one of the clearest indicators of where autonomous coding agents are heading.

### 6. Amazon makes AI-powered Alexa+ free on Fire TV, no Prime required
Amazon is dropping the paywall for Alexa+ on Fire TV devices, making its AI-powered assistant available free without a Prime subscription. The move dramatically expands the addressable audience for Alexa+ and turns Fire TV into a low-friction distribution channel for Amazon's consumer AI bets.

Amazon appears to be prioritizing adoption over subscription revenue as the AI assistant race heats up. Free, ubiquitous Alexa+ in the living room is a direct challenge to the assistant strategies of Google, Apple, and OpenAI on the biggest screen in the home.

### 7. TerraPower's nuclear reactor has a secret weapon for powering AI data centers
TechCrunch highlights TerraPower's next-generation nuclear reactor design as a potential key to powering AI data centers, with the company positioning its technology around the enormous and growing electricity demands of AI training and inference. TerraPower argues its approach offers faster, more predictable deployment than conventional nuclear projects.

Energy remains the binding constraint on AI buildout — land, power, and cooling decide where data centers go. If TerraPower and peers deliver on schedule, nuclear could move from aspirational to load-bearing for the AI infrastructure buildout over the next decade.
