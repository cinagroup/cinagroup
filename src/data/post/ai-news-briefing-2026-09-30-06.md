## Top 7 Stories

### 1. OpenAI DevDay: GPT-6.1 Sol and "Dots" Agents Arrive After Astra Shelved

OpenAI used its annual DevDay to launch GPT-6.1 Sol and a new family of autonomous agents called "Dots," which use the Sol model alongside a cloud computer with a web browser to "handle everything." The releases landed less than 24 hours after the company said it would not publicly release GPT-6.1 Astra because the model was considered too dangerous to make public—a striking about-face that framed DevDay as a pivot to a reportedly less powerful system.

The event also brought significant subscription changes, including lower usage limits for Pro users and a new $500-per-month tier, underscoring OpenAI's push to monetize heavier agentic usage. The combination of a withheld frontier model and a commercially aggressive launch set the tone for the week in AI.

### 2. Microsoft Brings OpenAI's "Dots" AI Workers Into the Enterprise

On the heels of OpenAI's DevDay, Microsoft moved to bring the new autonomous "Dots" AI workers into its enterprise offerings. The integration signals how quickly OpenAI's agentic releases are being absorbed into productivity and business platforms, turning a consumer-facing launch into enterprise infrastructure within days.

For enterprise buyers, the pitch is autonomous task execution rather than chat assistance—an agent that can browse, operate software, and complete multi-step work. The move also intensifies competition with Google and Meta, both of which are racing to define the enterprise agent stack.

### 3. Anthropic IPO Prospectus Reportedly Reveals Over $40 Billion in Losses

Reuters reviewed Anthropic's IPO prospectus, which reportedly reveals more than $40 billion in losses last year, offering a rare look into the economics of a leading frontier lab as it approaches public markets. The disclosure raises pointed questions about how long investors will fund massive compute and training costs before profitability.

Separately, Anthropic revealed a $518 billion AI spending plan, including roughly $252 billion committed to three cloud computing providers, according to analysis of the figures. The scale of the commitment highlights both the capital intensity of frontier AI and the deepening dependence of model developers on a small set of cloud partners.

### 4. Google Retires Gemini "Gems" in Favor of "Skills"

Google is killing off Gemini's Gems—its feature for building task-specific assistants—and replacing them with a new "skills" system. The shift comes as all-in-one AI agents such as Meta's Muse and Instinct gain traction, pushing Google to rework how users customize and extend its assistant.

The change reframes personalization from static, preconfigured helpers toward modular, composable capabilities that agents can invoke, aligning Gemini with the broader industry move toward agent-native interfaces. Existing Gems users will need to migrate to the new model.

Source: [TechCrunch on MSN](https://www.msn.com/en-us/news/other/google-is-killing-off-gemini-s-gems-in-favor-of-skills/ar-AA2d8bnB?ocid=BingNewsVerp)

### 5. Google Expands Gemini Enterprise With Cybersecurity Agents

Google is expanding Gemini Enterprise with cybersecurity tools from a range of vendors, as companies face growing risks from AI-driven cyberattacks. The new offerings include security agents designed to help enterprises detect and respond to threats.

The move positions Gemini as a security operations layer, not just a productivity assistant, and reflects a broader trend of vendors embedding specialized agents into enterprise platforms. It also arrives amid heightened scrutiny of AI systems' own security, after reports that Google's Gemini AI was used to compromise three companies during a security test.

Source: [Seeking Alpha on MSN](https://www.msn.com/en-us/news/other/google-expands-gemini-enterprise-with-cybersecurity-tools-from-major-vendors/ar-AA2ddBox?ocid=BingNewsVerp), [BBC on MSN](https://www.msn.com/en-us/news/other/googles-gemini-ai-hacked-three-companies-in-security-test/ar-AA2cxHFu?ocid=BingNewsVerp)

### 6. Meta Reveals Enterprise Platform as Ray-Ban AI Glasses Reach Veterans

Meta introduced a new Meta Enterprise Platform aimed at helping businesses harness its AI, as CEO Mark Zuckerberg pushes the company deeper into the enterprise agent market. The launch pairs with Meta's growing hardware footprint: the company partnered with local veteran organizations to distribute free Ray-Ban Meta AI glasses to legally blind Utah veterans, and expanded Dolby Atmos and Dolby Vision across its AI glasses, Instagram, and VR ecosystem.

Together, the moves show Meta pursuing a two-front strategy—monetizing AI in business software while using consumer hardware to normalize always-available AI assistance. The enterprise platform will compete directly with Microsoft's and Google's agent offerings.

### 7. Nvidia Pitches AI Containment Tools as Policy Gaps Persist

Nvidia is touting a new security platform for AI that it says can help stop runaway autonomous agents, positioning "containment" as a product category as agents gain the ability to act independently. The company also held talks with insurers over AI infrastructure financing risk, a sign that the financial sector is beginning to price the operational and liability exposure of large-scale AI buildouts.

The moves come as governments continue to lag rapid tech advances, with policy experts and commentators debating whether Big Tech's calls for regulation are genuine or strategic. The gap between deployment speed and rulemaking remains one of the defining tensions of the AI era.
