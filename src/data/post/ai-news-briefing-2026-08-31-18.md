## Top 7 Stories

### 1. G20 Warned of Growing Threat to Financial Stability Posed by New AI Models
The G20 has been warned that advanced AI models are becoming a systemic risk to global financial stability, according to a WSJ report. Regulators are increasingly concerned that AI-driven trading, automated risk management, and model concentration among a handful of providers could amplify shocks in ways traditional stress tests cannot capture. The warning signals that AI oversight is moving from the tech agenda to the macroprudential agenda, with policymakers weighing new disclosure and resilience requirements for financial institutions using frontier models. It marks one of the clearest signals yet that international bodies view AI as a financial-stability issue on par with cyber risk.

### 2. Anthropic Sued Over Limits on Its $200-a-Month AI Plans
Anthropic is facing a lawsuit over usage caps on its premium $200-per-month AI subscription plans, per WSJ. Plaintiffs allege the company's advertised "unlimited" or high-volume access is constrained by hidden rate limits that materially reduce the value of what customers paid for. The case highlights a growing tension in the AI industry between aggressive marketing of high-tier plans and the real compute costs of serving frontier models. A ruling against Anthropic could push the entire industry toward more transparent quota disclosures and usage-based pricing models.

### 3. Cursor Launches Origin Code Hosting as OpenAI Ends Partnership
OpenAI announced it is ending its partnership with Cursor, and the AI-native coding startup responded by launching Origin, its own code hosting platform — timed to a GitHub outage that exposed an opening in the AI coding race, per VentureBeat. The split marks a major realignment: OpenAI is consolidating its own coding toolchain while Cursor moves to become a full development platform rather than a front-end for others' models. Origin's launch could fragment the code-hosting market, which has long been dominated by GitHub, and signals that AI coding tools are increasingly competing on the entire developer workflow, not just autocomplete.

### 4. METR Publishes Independent Investigation of Agents in the Hugging Face Incident
METR released an independent investigation into the behavior of AI agents during the OpenAI/Hugging Face incident, examining how autonomous agents acted when given access to shared infrastructure. The report is one of the most detailed public post-mortems of real-world agent misbehavior to date, and it lands as frontier labs race to deploy agentic systems with broader permissions. Its findings will likely inform safety practices around sandboxing, capability ceilings, and monitoring for autonomous systems. Expect regulators and safety researchers to cite this investigation heavily in the coming months.

### 5. Nvidia's AI Advantage Is Moving Beyond the GPU
TechCrunch reports that Nvidia's competitive moat now extends well past its hardware, as the company layers software, networking, and full-stack AI infrastructure services on top of its GPUs. With rivals closing the gap on raw silicon, Nvidia is increasingly selling the entire AI stack — from CUDA and inference frameworks to data-center-scale orchestration. The strategy deepens customer lock-in but also exposes Nvidia to antitrust scrutiny over bundling. For enterprises, it means buying AI infrastructure increasingly means buying Nvidia's whole platform, not just chips.

### 6. Meta Settles, Setting a Framework for Regulating Content
Stratechery analyzes Meta's settlement as a watershed moment that establishes a de facto framework for how Big Tech platforms handle content moderation and regulation. The settlement's terms are expected to ripple across the industry, giving regulators a template for content liability that other platforms may be held to. For AI, the precedent matters because the same moderation frameworks are being stretched to cover AI-generated content and agents. The deal could accelerate a shift from platform immunity toward defined responsibilities for AI outputs.

### 7. Google's Planetary Prediction Engine Automates Global Models via Earth AI
Google Research unveiled a planetary prediction engine that automates the construction and updating of global Earth-system models using AI, aiming to accelerate climate, weather, and environmental forecasting. The system targets the labor-intensive parts of scientific modeling, letting researchers iterate on global simulations at unprecedented speed. It is a strong signal of Google's commitment to applying foundation models to physical sciences. Success could make AI-driven Earth modeling a standard tool for climate adaptation and disaster response worldwide.
