### 1. Anthropic Prices Biggest IPO on Record at $83 a Share
Sources: [AP](https://www.msn.com/en-us/news/other/anthropic-reportedly-prices-its-ipo-at-83-per-share-valuing-the-ai-giant-at-15-trillion/ar-AA2hJwe1) · [Axios](https://www.msn.com/en-us/money/economy/anthropic-prices-ipo-at-83-in-historic-1-trillion-offering/ar-AA2hJ2wK) · [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/anthropic-will-go-public-at-around-1-5-trillion-valuation-2026-11)

Anthropic priced its initial public offering at $83 per share — above its projected range — setting up a debut that press reports describe as the largest IPO on record, with a valuation reported in the $1–1.5 trillion range. Trading began this week amid intense investor demand, drawing comparisons to the most consequential market debuts of the decade.

The listing makes Anthropic the first of the frontier AI labs to reach public markets at scale, a milestone that shifts how the industry's capital-intensive training and compute bills will be scrutinized by shareholders. Coverage noted the offering's scale also raises fresh questions about token pricing and whether public-market pressure will push inference costs higher.

Analysts framed the IPO as a referendum on AI's commercial durability, with some enterprise-focused commentators counseling caution about how quickly revenue can justify the valuation. The debut will be watched closely as a proxy for the appetite surrounding rival labs' eventual listings.

### 2. Oregon Sues OpenAI for $2.035B Over Teen Safety
Sources: [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/oregon-slams-openai-with-new-chatbot-harm-kids-safety-suit) · [KOBI5](https://kobi5.com/news/top-stories/oregon-ag-launches-keeping-kids-safe-initiative-lawsuit-againt-openai-319924/) · [TechRepublic](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-oregon-openai-chatgpt-teen-safety-lawsuit/)

Oregon's attorney general filed a lawsuit against OpenAI seeking roughly $2.035 billion, accusing the company of child endangerment and of misleading the public about the safety of its chatbot for minors. The action was announced alongside a broader "Keeping Kids Safe" initiative from the state AG's office.

The suit is among the largest state-level actions yet targeting a frontier AI developer over consumer-harm theories, and it joins a growing docket of litigation arguing that chatbot design choices create foreseeable risks for young users. Bloomberg Law reported the filing frames the harm claims around product-safety and misrepresentation doctrines rather than novel AI-specific statutes.

OpenAI has not conceded the allegations. The case is expected to sharpen the debate over default guardrails and age-verification for consumer chatbots heading into 2027.

### 3. Lawsuit Alleges Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and SpaceXAI Agreed to 'Pace' AI Development
Sources: [AP](https://apnews.com/article/artificial-intelligence-ai-lawsuit-cyberattacks-anthropic-openai-03ece37b96979fe834f60976648504ba) · [Politico](https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/anthropic-openai-spacexai-google-sued-010440086.html) · [Verdict](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/ai-giants-sued-california-over-125933830.html)

Advocacy groups and subscribers filed suit in California against Anthropic, OpenAI, SpaceXAI and Google, alleging the companies made an illegal agreement to slow or "pace" AI development in a way that harms consumers and competition. The plaintiffs are asking the court to freeze new model releases, warning of "catastrophic" risks and alleging coordinated conduct.

The complaint centers on the labs' public rhetoric about slowing frontier development and whether that language crossed into collusive restraint. The defendants have characterized coordination on safety standards as responsible practice rather than anticompetitive behavior.

The case is one of several recent legal and regulatory fronts — including FTC scrutiny — testing how safety-motivated cooperation among labs will be treated under competition law.

### 4. Trump Administration Cleared Nearly 880,000 AI Chips for China in Six Months
Sources: [Reuters](https://www.msn.com/en-us/news/other/us-signed-off-on-sales-of-nearly-900-000-ai-chips-to-china-in-six-months-committee-says/ar-AA2hNWr8) · [CNBC](https://www.msn.com/en-us/money/markets/commerce-approved-sales-of-more-than-879000-ai-chips-to-chinese-companies-rep-mast-says/ar-AA2hNGm3) · [The Hill](https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/trump-administration-approved-shipment-nearly-214902852.html)

A House committee disclosed that the Commerce Department approved the export of 879,868 advanced AI chips to Chinese firms over roughly six months, per a disclosure first reported by Reuters and confirmed by CNBC. The figures cover Nvidia and AMD accelerators cleared under current licensing rules.

The disclosure re-energized a Washington debate over export-control policy, with lawmakers questioning whether the approvals are consistent with stated national-security objectives. Committee members cited the volume as evidence the current rules leave substantial room for sensitive hardware to reach China.

The Trump administration has defended the licensing framework as a calibrated approach that keeps US firms competitive while managing risk. Expect the committee's findings to feed into fresh legislative proposals on chip export limits.

### 5. OpenAI Ships GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol

OpenAI rolled out two new models — the flagship GPT-6.2 Astra and the cheaper GPT-6.2 Sol — across its product surface. Astra is positioned as the highest-capability model, while Sol targets cost-sensitive and high-throughput workloads.

Benchmark trackers placed Astra near the top on coding and 3D tasks, though independent testers were more mixed, with reports of unusual output quirks and a second model leading on some floor-plan style evaluations. Early adopters on social platforms offered sharply divided impressions of the cheaper variant.

OpenAI also described hardening Astra's defenses against reasoning-extraction attacks, part of an ongoing push to protect chain-of-thought internals from being probed out by third parties.

OpenAI extended ChatGPT for Healthcare with computer-use agents and 20+ plugins, and connected the product to Epic electronic health records, per TechRepublic and MobiHealthNews. Becker's reported the company expanding a set of three tools to healthcare customers as part of the rollout.

The move puts agentic workflows — systems that can operate software directly — in front of clinicians and hospital administrators, a category with strict privacy and accountability requirements. Integrations with EHR systems like Epic are a gateway for AI to act inside clinical operations rather than just answer questions.

The healthcare push is one of several vertical plays from OpenAI, which also recently launched ChatGPT for Sales with 30+ partner plugins. Regulators and health systems will be watching how the computer-use agents are scoped and audited.
