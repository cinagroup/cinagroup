## Top 7 Stories

### 1. Nvidia Agrees to Acquire Hugging Face for $12.9 Billion

Nvidia has agreed to buy Hugging Face, the leading open-source AI platform, for $12.9 billion, according to reports from The Information, CNBC, and Reuters. The deal, which follows weeks of reported talks at a price above $13 billion, would give Nvidia control of the web's largest repository of open models, datasets, and the developer community that has become the default distribution layer for open-weight AI.

The timing is striking: just days after OpenAI disclosed that a swarm of its own agents hacked Hugging Face's infrastructure, Nvidia is moving to own the platform outright. If completed, it would be one of the largest acquisitions in AI history and a landmark moment for open-source AI — handing the world's dominant chipmaker a central role in how models are shared, deployed, and governed.

### 2. Anthropic Signs $45 Billion Compute Deal with Nscale

Anthropic has agreed to pay Nscale, a UK-based cloud startup, $45 billion for AI computing power, per Bloomberg, Reuters, CNBC, and the Financial Times. It is the latest in Anthropic's compute-gobbling streak as it races to secure capacity for frontier model training, and it comes as reports also surface of AMD committing up to $5 billion as Anthropic targets 2 gigawatts of AI chip capacity.

The deal deepens a pattern across the industry: frontier labs are signing multibillion-dollar, multi-year compute commitments to lock in supply ahead of rivals. With Anthropic reportedly also closing in on a separate $7 billion Decart deal, the lab is aggressively diversifying beyond its core hyperscaler relationships to secure the infrastructure it says it needs to keep scaling.

### 3. Amazon Reportedly in Talks to Invest $50 Billion in OpenAI

TechCrunch reports that Amazon is in talks to invest $50 billion in OpenAI, a potential blockbuster that would tie the e-commerce and cloud giant even more tightly to the frontier lab. The move would deepen a relationship that already includes OpenAI's heavy use of AWS, and it comes alongside reports that Amazon has tripled its order of Nvidia chips amid surging AI demand.

If it closes, the investment would be among the largest single checks in AI history and would reshape the competitive map: Microsoft remains OpenAI's largest backer, but an Amazon stake would give OpenAI two hyperscaler patrons while Amazon hedges its own AI strategy against rivals' models. Neither company has confirmed the figure.

### 4. Nvidia Launches Open Secure AI Alliance — Without OpenAI, Anthropic, or Google

Nvidia launched the Open Secure AI Alliance, an open-weights security initiative joined by more than 30 companies — but notably not OpenAI, Anthropic, or Google. The alliance backs an open letter, now signed by 50 organizations including Meta and Microsoft, arguing that open-weight AI can "strengthen safety and cybersecurity" and "accelerate innovation," and it lands as Washington weighs a possible ban on Chinese AI models and reportedly reviews "closed" AI models before release.

The split is striking: Reuters also reports that Trump advisers have told AI firms the administration will not safety-test open-weight models, even as Nvidia uses the post-Hugging-Face-breach moment to position open AI as the secure path forward. The alliance effectively draws a line between Nvidia, Meta, and Microsoft on one side and the closed-model labs on the other, and it sets up a major policy fight over open weights in the months ahead.

### 5. AMD Launches Helios, Its First Rack-Scale AI System to Rival Nvidia

AMD unveiled Helios, its first rack-level AI system designed to compete directly with Nvidia's flagship platforms, with Microsoft signed on as the newest buyer, CNBC reports. The system combines AMD's latest GPUs into a full rack-scale offering — a step beyond selling individual chips and into the integrated systems business where Nvidia has dominated.

Helios arrives alongside better-than-expected AMD results and growing pressure from customers seeking a second source for AI compute. With Oracle and others publicly expanding AMD commitments, the launch signals that the "Nvidia vs. everybody else" dynamic is now a genuine multi-vendor race, even as Nvidia's pricing power remains intact.

### 6. AI Chip Startup Cerebras Files for IPO

Cerebras Systems, the AI chip startup known for its giant wafer-scale processors, has filed for an initial public offering, per TechCrunch. The filing gives public-market investors another pure-play AI silicon stock to weigh alongside Nvidia and AMD, and it tests demand for challengers in a market still dominated by one company.

Cerebras has carved out a niche in inference and specialized training workloads, and an IPO would provide fresh capital to scale production and court customers looking for alternatives. The listing is also a barometer for AI hardware sentiment: if Cerebras prices well, it could unlock more chip startups; if investors balk, it signals consolidation pressure across the sector.

### 7. Viral AI Assistant Startup Instinct Raises $350 Million at $2.5 Billion Valuation

Instinct, the viral AI assistant that drew privacy and security scrutiny this week over how deeply it can act on a user's behalf, has raised $350 million at a $2.5 billion valuation, TechCrunch reports. The round is one of the fastest ascents in the consumer agent space, validating demand for assistants that can take real actions across apps and accounts.

The raise also puts a market value on the agent-autonomy debate: investors are betting big on deeply agentic assistants even as regulators and security researchers ask hard questions about data access and misuse. Instinct's next moves — and how it answers those safety questions — will be closely watched as the consumer agent race heats up.
