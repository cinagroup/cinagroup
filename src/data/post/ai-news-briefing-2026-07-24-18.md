## Top 7 Stories

### 1. OpenAI Launches ChatGPT Health to All U.S. Users

OpenAI officially made ChatGPT Health available to every user in the United States this week, marking the company's most ambitious push into consumer healthcare. The feature, which had been in limited testing, offers personalized health guidance, symptom triage, and wellness recommendations powered by GPT-5-level models fine-tuned on medical literature. OpenAI claims the tool already outperforms general practitioners on diagnostic benchmarks, though the company is careful to position it as informational rather than a substitute for doctors. The launch puts OpenAI in direct competition with established telehealth platforms and raises fresh questions about AI regulation in medicine.

### 2. AMD Commits Up to $5 Billion to Anthropic

AMD announced a landmark deal to invest up to $5 billion in Anthropic, securing a strategic partnership that covers cloud compute, custom silicon, and joint infrastructure development. The multi-year agreement positions AMD as Anthropic's primary hardware partner and intensifies the AI arms race with NVIDIA, which has long dominated the AI training and inference market. The deal also includes co-engineering next-generation AI chips optimized for Claude's architecture. For Anthropic, the partnership diversifies its compute supply chain beyond the major cloud hyperscalers and signals that the AI lab is preparing for an enormous scale-up in model training capacity.

### 3. NVIDIA Is Sending GPUs to the Moon

In one of the most unexpected AI infrastructure stories of the year, NVIDIA confirmed plans to deploy GPU-accelerated computing hardware on the lunar surface as part of a multi-phase collaboration with space agencies and private aerospace firms. The initiative aims to enable on-site AI processing for autonomous lunar rovers, habitat systems, and scientific instruments — avoiding the latency and bandwidth constraints of Earth-based compute. While details remain sparse, the announcement underscores how AI hardware is becoming mission-critical infrastructure far beyond data centers, and positions NVIDIA as a key player in the emerging space economy.

### 4. Google Gemini Nears One Billion Users

Google's Gemini AI platform is closing in on the billion-user milestone, a threshold previously reached only by a handful of the company's flagship products like Search, Gmail, and YouTube. The rapid growth has been fueled by Gemini's deep integration across Android, Workspace, and Google Cloud, as well as aggressive international expansion. The milestone reinforces Google's bet that embedding AI directly into existing products — rather than treating it as a standalone chatbot — is the fastest path to mass adoption. It also intensifies the competition with OpenAI's ChatGPT, which remains the market leader in dedicated AI assistant usage.

### 5. Anthropic Brings Claude Voice Mode to Opus and Sonnet

Anthropic updated Claude's voice mode to support its most powerful models, Opus and Sonnet, giving users natural spoken conversation with the company's top-tier AI. Previously limited to the faster but less capable Haiku model, voice mode now delivers the depth and reasoning power of Anthropic's flagship systems in real-time spoken interactions. The move closes a feature gap with OpenAI's ChatGPT Voice and positions Claude as a more versatile daily assistant. Early reviews praise the natural prosody and low latency, though availability is currently rolling out gradually across platforms.

### 6. Lawmakers Prepare Bill Requiring AI "Kill Switch"

A bipartisan group of U.S. lawmakers is drafting legislation that would require AI developers to build mandatory "kill switch" mechanisms into advanced AI systems, allowing operators — or regulators — to rapidly shut down models deemed dangerous or out of control. The proposal, expected to be formally introduced in the coming weeks, represents one of the most direct federal interventions into AI safety to date. Critics argue the bill is overly broad and could stifle innovation, while supporters say it is a necessary safeguard as frontier models grow increasingly autonomous and capable. The legislation is likely to face fierce debate in both houses.

### 7. AI Chip Startup Etched Hits $10.3 Billion Valuation

Etched, the audacious AI chip startup building transformer-specific ASICs, has reached a $10.3 billion valuation in its latest funding round — defying skeptics who questioned whether a single-architecture chip could compete in the rapidly evolving AI hardware market. Backed by prominent Silicon Valley investors, the company claims its Sohu chip delivers orders-of-magnitude better performance-per-watt than general-purpose GPUs for transformer inference workloads. The valuation signals that investors see room for specialized AI silicon alongside the dominant GPU ecosystem, and that the market is big enough to support multiple hardware approaches as inference demand skyrockets.
