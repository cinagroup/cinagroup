## Top 7 Stories

### 1. Nvidia's Jensen Huang Declares 'AGI Has Arrived' With GPT-6 Astra
Business Insider reports that Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang said AGI "has arrived" while congratulating OpenAI on GPT-6 Astra, with Yahoo Finance and Investing.com carrying similar remarks over the weekend. Huang's declaration — that the milestone is effectively behind the industry — lands days after the model's rollout and at the peak of the frenzy around OpenAI's agentic flagship.

The claim is already drawing sharp pushback. AI researcher Gary Marcus argues Huang offered "no evidence and no definitions," and the exchange sharpens a long-running split between industry executives who treat AGI as a practical threshold and critics who call it marketing. For policymakers and investors, the question is no longer hypothetical: if a chipmaker's CEO declares AGI here, what does that do to the safety debate and to the regulatory clock?

### 2. OpenAI Caps Launch Week With 'GPT-6 Astra: A New Generation of Intelligence' Post
OpenAI published "GPT-6 Astra: A new generation of intelligence" on Monday, framing the model as a step-change in what AI systems can do in work settings rather than a routine point release. Notebookcheck adds that Astra is now available on ChatGPT Plus — but only in the Work and Codex experiences, a deliberate gating that keeps the most capable agentic features inside productivity contexts.

The post arrives amid what Al Jazeera describes as "rising scrutiny and safety concerns" around the model, and one week after CEO Sam Altman apologized for a rollout that temporarily locked out paying users. The framing matters: OpenAI is positioning Astra as a "generation" rather than a version, which raises the stakes for rivals and gives regulators a crisper target as agent-related incidents continue to dominate Washington's attention.

### 3. Google to Build New AI Chip in Israel, Tapping Hailo Talent
Haaretz reports that Google will build a new AI chip in Israel, tapping talent from Hailo, the Israeli edge-AI chipmaker. The move extends Google's custom-silicon push — already visible in its reported Marvell partnership and TPU roadmap — into a new geography with deep chip engineering expertise.

Building AI silicon in Israel gives Google a hedge in a supply chain increasingly shaped by export controls and geopolitical risk, and it puts the company in direct competition for scarce chip talent with Nvidia and the cloud rivals. The story also signals that the hyperscaler chip arms race is broadening beyond data-center design into where, and with whom, those chips get built.

### 4. Reuters Breakingviews: China's AI Dragons Breathe Fire on Nvidia's Moat
Reuters Breakingviews argues that China's AI champions are starting to threaten Nvidia's dominance, as domestic silicon and model stacks — including DeepSeek's rapid ascent — chip away at the assumption that the CUDA ecosystem is unassailable. The commentary lands as Nvidia balances record demand against tightening export rules and a reported China-specific chip plan that the company has denied.

The analysis cuts to the central strategic question of the AI boom: whether Nvidia's moat is technological or merely temporal. If Chinese labs can close the gap with less advanced hardware, the pricing power that underpins Nvidia's valuation faces its first credible test — and the geopolitics of AI compute become inseparable from the chipmaker's earnings.

### 5. Cathay Pacific and Google Expand AI Trials to Cut Climate-Warming Contrails
Reuters and Bloomberg report that Cathay Pacific and Google are expanding their AI-driven trials to reduce aircraft contrails, the thin clouds that trap heat and account for a significant slice of aviation's climate impact. The program uses Google's AI to suggest altitude changes that avoid contrail-forming conditions, building on earlier proof-of-concept work.

The expansion is a rare unambiguously positive AI-climate story in a week dominated by data-center energy angst. It also gives Google a tangible demonstration of AI delivering real-world emissions cuts — valuable cover as the company's own AI infrastructure comes under fire for its power footprint.

### 6. FT: AI Computing Demand May Never Be Sated, Says Nvidia Partner Iren
The Financial Times reports that the CEO of Iren, an Italian utility and Nvidia partner, says AI computing demand "may never be sated," as power constraints become the binding constraint on data-center growth. The comments underscore how electricity — not chips — is increasingly the bottleneck for the AI buildout.

The story connects directly to the policy fights of recent weeks, including the pushback against coal-powered data centers and the scramble for grid capacity around major AI hubs. For anyone tracking the AI trade, power is now the tell: whoever secures reliable, cheap electricity holds the real option on the next wave of compute.

### 7. Morgan Stanley and Goldman Near Top Roles on Anthropic's Mega-IPO
Private Banker International reports that Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs are closing in on top roles for Anthropic's blockbuster IPO, as the company's filing — expected as soon as this month — nears. Morningstar, meanwhile, published a detailed look at why the listing, reportedly targeting a $2 trillion valuation, carries unusual risks for investors, from governance structure to concentration of revenue in a few partners.

The banker lineup is the latest concrete sign that the offering is moving from speculation to execution. With Anthropic's unusual trustee-governed structure, its reported $100 billion-plus AWS commitment, and the sheer size of the raise, the prospectus is set to become the most scrutinized document in tech — and the underwriting mandates are the first visible spoils of the race to lead it.
