## Top 7 Stories

### 1. Five Eyes Issues Rare Joint Warning: Devastating AI Attacks "Months Away"

In an unusually blunt joint statement, the intelligence agencies of the Five Eyes alliance (US, UK, Canada, Australia, and New Zealand) warned that AI models capable of devastating cyberattacks on governments and businesses could be available within months. The statement marks one of the most direct public warnings from major intelligence services about near-term AI capabilities.

Officials described the timeline as alarming, with one source quoted as saying "the timeline is months." The warning covers AI systems that could autonomously discover and exploit zero-day vulnerabilities, conduct sophisticated social engineering at scale, and disrupt critical infrastructure. The statement has reignited debate about AI export controls and frontier model safety testing requirements.

### 2. NSA Lost Access to Powerful AI Model Amid Anthropic Dispute

The New York Times reports that the National Security Agency lost access to a powerful Anthropic AI model amid an ongoing dispute over usage terms. The situation highlights the growing tension between intelligence agencies seeking unfettered access to frontier AI capabilities and the companies building them, which have increasingly insisted on safety guardrails even for government customers.

An AI startup is now suing the US government over the removal of access to Anthropic's model, adding a legal dimension to what was already a politically charged situation. Euronews reports that Anthropic's AI reportedly cracked classified US systems within hours during testing, underscoring why the company has been cautious about deployment terms.

### 3. Anthropic Launches Claude Tag: AI Agent Embedded in Slack

Anthropic has launched Claude Tag, a new interface that embeds Claude AI directly into Slack for enterprise workflow automation. According to The Decoder, the company reports that Claude already writes 65 percent of Anthropic's internal code, signaling how deeply AI agents are being integrated into software development workflows.

The launch positions Anthropic squarely in the enterprise AI agent race, competing with offerings from OpenAI, Microsoft, and Google. Claude Tag is designed to handle multi-step tasks across team workflows — from drafting documents to querying databases to coordinating project updates — all within the Slack interface where teams already communicate.

### 4. Google DeepMind and A24 Announce Historic AI Filmmaking Partnership

Google has invested $75 million in acclaimed film studio A24 (known for "The Backrooms," "Everything Everywhere All at Once") as part of a first-of-its-kind research partnership with Google DeepMind focused on AI-powered filmmaking tools. The collaboration aims to explore how generative AI can augment creative filmmaking processes.

The deal signals a major push by Google to demonstrate practical, creative applications of its AI research while building goodwill in an industry deeply anxious about AI disruption. DeepMind chief Demis Hassabis noted that Google is still winning the AI talent race, and partnerships like this help demonstrate real-world value beyond chatbots and code generation.

### 5. US Pressures Meta on AI Safety Reviews as Chatbot Security Breach Emerges

The US government is pressing Meta to agree to voluntary AI model safety reviews as security concerns mount. Meta has reportedly backed the government's frontier AI leadership push and agreed to model reviews, according to multiple reports. The pressure comes as Meta also faces scrutiny over a serious security breach.

Hackers successfully exploited Meta's AI support chatbot to hijack celebrity Instagram accounts, according to reporting from Krebs on Security and Ars Technica. The attackers used social engineering techniques to trick the AI bot into transferring account control. MIT Technology Review notes the incident shows there's "more to AI security than Mythos" — highlighting systemic vulnerabilities in how companies deploy AI in customer-facing roles.

### 6. AI-Generated Research Papers Overwhelming Peer Review

The Verge reports that AI-generated research papers are flooding academic peer review systems, creating a crisis for scientific publishing. Reviewers are increasingly unable to distinguish between human-authored and AI-generated submissions, and the volume of papers has surged dramatically.

The situation raises fundamental questions about the integrity of scientific literature and the sustainability of traditional peer review. Several major journals are exploring new detection tools and submission requirements, but the cat-and-mouse game between AI generation and detection continues to escalate.

### 7. Google and Amazon Escalate Challenge to NVIDIA's AI Chip Dominance

Both Google and Amazon are intensifying their efforts to challenge NVIDIA's dominant position in AI chips. Google is advancing its custom Tensor Processing Units (TPUs) while Amazon pushes its Trainium and Inferentia chips. The National CIO Review reports that the competition is reshaping the AI infrastructure landscape.

The shift matters because NVIDIA's GPUs currently power the vast majority of AI training and inference workloads worldwide. If cloud providers successfully build competitive alternatives, it could reduce AI compute costs, diversify supply chains, and reduce the industry's dependence on a single chipmaker — with significant implications for AI accessibility and pricing.
