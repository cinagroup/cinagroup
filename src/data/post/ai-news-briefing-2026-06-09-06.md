## Top 7 Stories

### 1. Anthropic Overtakes OpenAI as World's Most Valuable AI Startup at $965 Billion

Anthropic has surpassed OpenAI to become the most valuable artificial intelligence company in the world, reaching a valuation of approximately $965 billion in its latest funding round — putting it within striking distance of the $1 trillion mark. The milestone, reported by CNBC, The Guardian, and Axios, marks a dramatic reversal of fortunes: just months ago OpenAI held the crown as the industry's most prized private company.

The shift reflects surging enterprise demand for Anthropic's Claude models, particularly in coding and agentic workflows, where the company has pulled ahead in developer benchmarks. Investors are betting that Anthropic's safety-first branding and strong government contracts give it a durable moat as AI spending accelerates across industries.

### 2. Google Debuts New AI Models and Personal AI Agents to Challenge OpenAI and Anthropic

Google unveiled a slate of new Gemini AI models alongside personal AI agents designed to handle everyday tasks — a direct response to mounting pressure from Anthropic and OpenAI. The announcements, covered by CNBC and WSJ, include a new Gemini agent capable of managing calendars, drafting emails, and navigating apps on a user's behalf.

The company is also rebranding its AI Search experience, signaling a broader push to embed agentic AI throughout its product ecosystem. Google's internal challenge remains acute: a Los Angeles Times report reveals the company is losing ground in the AI coding race to Anthropic and OpenAI, making this week's launches a critical counteroffensive.

### 3. Microsoft Unveils In-House AI Models to Reduce Reliance on OpenAI

Microsoft announced new proprietary AI models designed to lower costs for developers and reduce the company's dependence on OpenAI — a striking shift in a partnership that has defined the AI era. The move, reported by CNBC, signals that even the closest OpenAI alliance is evolving as Microsoft seeks more control over its AI stack.

At its Build 2026 conference, Microsoft also unveiled "Scout," a new personal work agent powered by its OpenClaw framework. The dual strategy — building in-house while maintaining the OpenAI relationship — reflects the industry's broader tension between collaboration and competition at the frontier.

### 4. Pentagon Tests Rival AI Models in Race to Replace Anthropic

The U.S. Department of Defense is actively testing competing AI models as part of an effort to reduce its reliance on Anthropic, Bloomberg reports. The initiative comes amid an ongoing dispute between the Pentagon and the AI safety leader over contract terms and model access.

The development has drawn sharp reactions: hundreds of Google and OpenAI employees have publicly backed Anthropic in the dispute, according to The Hill. Meanwhile, DefenseScoop reports the DOD has expanded classified AI work with eight other companies — notably excluding Anthropic — highlighting the growing fragmentation of AI in national security.

### 5. OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google Unite to Combat AI Model Theft in China

In a rare show of unity, OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google are collaborating to fight the copying of their AI models by Chinese competitors, according to Bloomberg and MSN. The joint effort targets systematic intellectual property theft that has allowed rival labs to replicate frontier capabilities at a fraction of the training cost.

The coalition represents a significant shift: companies that compete fiercely in the market are aligning on IP protection and security. The move comes as Alibaba's AI models have begun beating Google and OpenAI in global coding rankings, according to the South China Morning Post, underscoring the urgency of the threat.

### 6. NVIDIA Ships First PCs Designed Specifically for AI Agents

NVIDIA introduced the first personal computers designed from the ground up for AI agents, the Wall Street Journal reports. The new hardware, developed in partnership with Microsoft, represents a fundamental rethinking of the PC for an era where autonomous software — not human users — is the primary workload.

The launch coincides with NVIDIA's broader push into "physical AI," including new agent skills for autonomous vehicles, robotics, and vision AI. Combined with Microsoft's Build 2026 announcements about AI-driven devices, the news signals that the AI-agent PC category is moving from concept to commercial reality.

### 7. AI Policy Accelerates: Trump Directive, EU AI Act Deadline, and Meta's WhatsApp Battle

The regulatory landscape for AI is intensifying on multiple fronts. President Trump signed a new directive aimed at boosting AI innovation and cybersecurity, while U.S. companies face the EU AI Act's possible August 2026 compliance deadline. Meanwhile, the EU has warned Meta over its policy of charging rival AI chatbots for WhatsApp access, with regulators arguing the fee structure may violate competition rules.

Meta is attempting to avoid EU fines by offering free WhatsApp access for rival AI chatbots under certain conditions, Reuters reports. The overlapping pressures — U.S. deregulation, European enforcement, and antitrust scrutiny of AI agents — are creating a complex compliance environment that will shape how AI companies operate globally through the rest of 2026.
