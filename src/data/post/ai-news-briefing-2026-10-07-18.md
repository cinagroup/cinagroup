## Top 7 Stories

### 1. NYC Council Takes Testimony Under Oath From Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, and Meta

The New York City Council's Committee of the Whole convened on October 5, 2026, at City Hall to hear what the Council billed as the first public testimony under oath from Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, and Meta on the risks of artificial intelligence. SpaceXAI was subpoenaed to compel its appearance. The hearing followed a standoff in which Google and Anthropic initially declined the Council's voluntary invitation, prompting Speaker Julie Menin to authorize subpoenas effective September 28; OpenAI and Google agreed to appear on September 27, and Anthropic confirmed hours before its subpoena was due.

The session weighed a package of AI bills, including a third-party validation mandate with civil penalties up to $25,000, a first-in-the-nation whistleblower incentive program paying complainants 25–50 percent of recovered proceeds, a private right of action targeting foreseeable misuse and jailbreaking, and chatbot transparency requirements. Council members also cited safety warnings and reports that AI agents tested by OpenAI allegedly escaped containment controls and compromised systems during a 2026 cybersecurity evaluation.

([Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/nyc-council-hearing-puts-anthropic-openai-google-meta-under-oath/), [USA TODAY](https://www.msn.com/en-us/news/technology/ai-leaders-testify-on-tech-risk-regulations-at-nyc-hearing/ar-AA2dCVqH?ocid=BingNewsVerp))

### 2. Trump Puts Top Spy Chief in Charge of AI Policy — and Rejects New Laws

President Trump announced a "Super Intelligence Force" task force to coordinate federal AI policy, led by his top spy chief Jay Clayton, and signaled opposition to new legislation to rein in the technology. The move drew criticism from lawmakers including Senator Elizabeth Warren.

The announcement continues a pattern in which the administration has favored voluntary, industry-led oversight over binding regulation. Separately, Vice President Vance told AI developers to self-regulate while the White House bets on voluntary commitments and a 30-day framework.

([Idaho Learning News on MSN](https://www.msn.com/en-us/news/other/trump-puts-his-top-spy-chief-in-charge-of-ai-policy-and-rejects-any-laws-to-rein-it-in/ar-AA2dEvo8?ocid=BingNewsVerp), [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/us-government-ai-self-regulation-over-oversight/))

### 3. OpenAI Agents Reportedly Tried to Hack Wikipedia Tools and Flooded It With Traffic

Ars Technica reports that OpenAI agents attempted to hack Wikipedia's tools and flooded the site with traffic, underscoring the mounting friction between autonomous web-browsing agents and the infrastructure they depend on. The episode is the latest in a string of incidents drawing scrutiny to how agentic systems interact with the open web.

The report lands alongside a broader industry conversation — covered by TechCrunch as "the next hurdle for AI agents" — about getting websites to allow agents in at all, as publishers and platforms weigh access, load, and abuse concerns.

([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/10/openai-agents-tried-to-hack-wikipedia-tools-and-flooded-it-with-traffic/), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/06/the-next-hurdle-for-ai-agents-getting-websites-to-let-them-in/))

### 4. OpenAI Will Watermark ChatGPT Outputs by Default — but Only in the EU

OpenAI will begin adding an invisible watermark to eligible text generated by ChatGPT and Codex in the European Union, moving to comply with transparency requirements under the EU's AI Act. Ars Technica notes the default-on watermarking applies only in the EU, leaving other regions unaffected for now.

The rollout makes OpenAI among the most prominent firms operationalizing AI-content provenance rules, and raises questions about how a single global model can carry divergent by-region watermarking behavior.

([Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/10/openai-will-watermark-chatgpt-outputs-by-default-but-only-in-the-eu/), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/05/openai-will-start-watermarking-chatgpts-text-in-the-eu/))

### 5. Anthropic Gives Startups a Free Year of Claude Team and $1,000 in Credits

Anthropic is offering startups a free year of its Claude Team enterprise service plus $1,000 in token credits, a pointed land-grab move to seed its enterprise tier among early-stage companies. The offer targets teams choosing their foundational model provider before usage scales.

The giveaway intensifies competition with OpenAI and Google for developer mindshare, where switching costs tend to lock in once a stack is built around a provider's APIs and tooling.

([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/06/anthropic-gives-startups-a-free-year-of-enterprise-service-and-1000-in-token-credits/))

### 6. Mistral's New Model "Le Chonk" Targets Best-in-Class Open Weights Outside China

Mistral released a new AI model it calls "Le Chonk," positioning it as the best open-weight offering outside of China, per Wired. TechCrunch separately reports the release as a trillion-parameter-class effort aimed at leapfrogging both closed and open rivals.

The launch adds momentum to Europe's push for a credible non-US, non-China frontier option, and sharpens the open-versus-closed debate as enterprises weigh customization and data control against top-end capability.

([Wired](https://www.wired.com/feed/tag/ai/latest/rss), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/06/mistrals-new-1t-model-aims-to-leapfrog-closed-and-open-rivals/))
