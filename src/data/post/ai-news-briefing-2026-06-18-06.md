## Top 7 Stories

### 1. G7 Summit: AI CEOs Join World Leaders to Shape AI Governance

CEOs from Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google DeepMind attended the G7 summit alongside President Trump and other world leaders, marking an unprecedented gathering of AI industry heads with government officials. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei and Google DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis called for a U.S.-led international AI coalition to coordinate on safety standards and governance frameworks. The meeting signals a shift toward closer government-industry collaboration on AI policy at the highest levels.

### 2. OpenAI and Anthropic File for IPOs in the Same Week

In a historic move, both OpenAI and Anthropic filed for initial public offerings within the same week, fundamentally changing the AI industry's landscape. OpenAI filed first, followed quickly by Anthropic, setting up what analysts are calling the most significant tech IPO race in years. The dual filings reflect the massive capital needs of frontier AI development and the industry's transition from venture-funded startups to public companies.

### 3. Anthropic Surpasses OpenAI as World's Most Valuable AI Startup

Anthropic has overtaken OpenAI to become the world's most valuable AI startup, according to The New York Times. The valuation leap comes as Anthropic's Claude models have gained significant market share, particularly in enterprise deployments. The company has also signed over 12 data center leases and is seeking $40 billion in backing from Google, signaling aggressive expansion plans ahead of its IPO.

### 4. AI Price Wars Erupt Among OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google

A fierce price war has broken out among the frontier AI labs, with OpenAI reportedly seeking major price cuts to compete with Anthropic, according to the Wall Street Journal. The competition is driving down API costs and subscription prices across the industry, benefiting developers and enterprises but putting pressure on margins. Sherwood News reports this represents a fundamental shift in how AI companies compete — moving from capability races to affordability battles.

### 5. Trump Signs AI Safety Executive Order

President Trump signed an executive order on AI safety that seeks voluntary review of new frontier models before deployment. The order calls for frontier model security assessments, early government access to new systems, and AI-enabled cyber defense capabilities. The move represents a more collaborative approach to AI regulation compared to previous administrations, relying on industry cooperation rather than mandatory requirements.

### 6. EU Publishes Draft Guidelines on High-Risk AI Systems Under AI Act

The European Commission published draft guidelines clarifying which AI systems qualify as high-risk under the EU AI Act. The guidelines provide much-needed specificity for companies developing AI systems in healthcare, finance, law enforcement, and other sensitive domains. Jones Day reports the draft clarifies classification criteria and compliance obligations, though industry groups continue to push for more flexible implementation timelines.

### 7. U.S. AI Export Controls Spark International Controversy

U.S. AI export controls are causing significant friction with allies, according to the Center for European Policy Analysis. The restrictions on advanced AI chip exports and model sharing have raised concerns among European and Asian partners about being locked out of cutting-edge AI development. The controversy comes as the G7 summit highlighted the tension between national security concerns and the desire for collaborative AI development.
