### 1. NVIDIA's board weighs Jensen Huang's future as spy scandal escalates

Sources: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/11/nvidias-stock-drops-as-board-reportedly-preps-to-oust-ceo-jensen-huang/) · [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/12/jensen-huang-nvidia-affair-ceo.html) · [The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/11/technology/nvidia-jensen-huang.html)

NVIDIA's board of directors spent Wednesday evening deliberating CEO Jensen Huang's future, according to reports, after disclosures that Huang had a relationship with an NVIDIA executive who reports to him — and that the woman at the center of the story is tied to Megaspeed, an NVIDIA distribution partner under scrutiny for possible diversion of chips to China. The New York Times reported that the board is weighing Huang's position; CNBC described a board meeting that stretched late into the evening.

Markets reacted sharply. NVIDIA shares fell roughly 6% as investors awaited word on Huang's fate, with CNBC quoting analysts calling it "a dark day for NVIDIA." TechCrunch reported the stock drop as the board reportedly prepared for the possibility of removing its founder-CEO.

The story has quickly become a policy matter: Congress is preparing to call Huang to testify, and the affair has revived a push to tighten China chip export curbs ahead of a Trump–Xi summit. Silicon Valley allies have publicly backed Huang, while the NYT reported on internal administration clashes over Megaspeed.

### 2. OpenAI's quarterly revenue reportedly crosses $10B for the first time

Sources: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/11/openais-quarterly-revenue-reportedly-crosses-10b-for-the-first-time/) · [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-10/openai-third-quarter-revenue-soars-nearly-50-to-10-billion) · [The Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/openais-second-quarter-sales-show-tepid-growth-compared-with-anthropic-5cb42998)

OpenAI's quarterly revenue crossed $10 billion for the first time, Bloomberg reported, citing anonymous sources — an almost 50% jump from the $6.7 billion recorded in the second quarter. TechCrunch, citing the Bloomberg report, said the company has reached an annual revenue run rate of roughly $50 billion.

Profitability also improved: OpenAI's adjusted operating margin narrowed from -150% in Q2 to -109% in Q3, according to the report. The rebound follows a period in which OpenAI's growth rate had slowed to about 18% quarter-over-quarter while rival Anthropic pulled ahead in enterprise revenue.

The comparison remains stark. Anthropic reported Q3 revenue of $17 billion and said its annual run rate climbed by $10 billion in a month to $81 billion as of the end of October, as it seeks a $1.4 trillion valuation in an imminent IPO. The two companies calculate annualized revenue differently — Anthropic counts cloud-partner sales, while OpenAI reports only its share of some partner revenue — a caveat worth keeping in mind when reading the headline numbers.

### 3. TSMC to invest $1B each in Anthropic and OpenAI

Sources: [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/11/taiwans-chipmaking-giant-tsmc-to-invest-1b-in-anthropic-openai/) · [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/11/openais-quarterly-revenue-reportedly-crosses-10b-for-the-first-time/) · [The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/10/technology/anthropic-ipo-google-amazon.html)

TSMC plans to take $1 billion stakes in both Anthropic and OpenAI, Ars Technica reported, describing the move as the latest example of the AI boom's circular financing — chipmakers investing in the model labs whose compute demands drive their own order books. The investments land as the AI trade wobbles and as both labs prepare for or weigh major capital-market events.

The deal deepens TSMC's entanglement with its largest AI customers at a moment when the foundry is already the single point of failure for advanced AI silicon. It also gives the labs another aligned supplier-shareholder as they compete for constrained capacity.

The NYT separately reported that Amazon and Google stand to see large paydays from Anthropic's IPO, underscoring how thoroughly the compute supply chain and the model layer have become financially interlocked. Analysts have flagged such arrangements as a governance concern, since they can inflate apparent demand and blur the line between customer and investor.

### 4. Google DeepMind unveils Gemini Robotics 2.5 and an open robot-safety benchmark

Sources: [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/google/2026/11/deepmind-reveals-gemini-robotics-2-5-and-open-safety-benchmark-asimov-jailbreak/) · [Google DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/) · [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/category/artificial-intelligence/)

Google DeepMind released Gemini Robotics 2.5, a vision-language-action model the company says is markedly better at tasks requiring high dexterity. Evaluated across more than 200 activities — including serving food with tongs and using a lab pipette — the model showed a 30% to 100% higher success rate than the previous version and ran 20% to 30% faster, per Ars Technica.

The most striking demonstration: Apptronik's Apollo 2 humanoid, powered by the new model, can tie a bow with five-fingered hands — a notable result because deformable objects like ribbon are notoriously hard for robots to manipulate. In a real-world test, Gemini-powered Apollo 2 robots kitted automotive parts at European Mercedes-Benz factories with a 96% success rate. DeepMind director Kanishka Rao acknowledged that closing the remaining gap to human reliability is a "steep road."

DeepMind also published ASIMOV-Jailbreak, an open-source dataset of 1,500 prompts designed to trick robotic AI into unsafe behavior, available on Hugging Face. DeepMind says placing its Argon simulation model in the loop yields a 98.8% jailbreak-safety score, versus 73.5% with the reasoning model alone. Gemini Robotics 2.5 is not generally available but is in the hands of partners including Agile Robots, Apptronik, and Boston Dynamics.

### 5. Google DeepMind mathematically verifies key parts of Signal's encryption

Sources: [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/11/signals-encryption-just-passed-an-ai-powered-mathematical-checkup/) · [Google DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/) · [Wired](https://www.wired.com/feed/tag/ai/latest/rss)

Google DeepMind used AI-assisted formal verification to mathematically check key components of Signal's end-to-end encryption, Ars Technica reported — a rare case of automated theorem-proving tooling being applied to production cryptography rather than toy problems. The work speaks to a growing research thrust: using AI to generate and check formal proofs at a scale human auditors cannot match.

For Signal, the result is a stronger assurance story than code review alone can provide, though formal verification covers specified properties rather than the entire protocol stack. For the wider field, it is a concrete data point in the debate over whether AI systems can be trusted to reason rigorously about security-critical code.

The result arrives as AI-assisted code generation spreads rapidly through developer tooling, raising the stakes for automated correctness checking. If AI can verify, it becomes more defensible to let AI write.

### 6. Meta loses its longtime head of infrastructure

Sources: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/11/meta-is-losing-its-longtime-head-of-infrastructure/) · [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/11/does-meta-think-we-cant-read-meta-abandons-sketchy-commercial-terms-update/) · [Wired](https://www.wired.com/feed/tag/ai/latest/rss)

Meta is losing its longtime head of infrastructure, TechCrunch reported, a significant departure at a company spending tens of billions of dollars a year on AI data centers and custom silicon. The role oversees the physical and networking foundation for Meta's training clusters and inference fleet, making it one of the most operationally consequential jobs in the company.

The exit comes amid broader turbulence in Meta's AI organization and a public-relations misstep: Ars Technica reported that Meta abandoned a controversial update to its commercial terms after users pointed out the language appeared to permit expansive use of their content — under the headline "Does Meta think we can't read?"

Infrastructure leadership churn matters more than most executive departures because AI buildouts are bottlenecked on execution: power contracts, construction timelines, and chip allocation all depend on continuity in exactly this function.

### 7. AI agents are leaking secrets: Grok Build and Claude Code incidents

Sources: [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/11/grok-build-coding-agent-leaks-oodles-of-confidential-info-some-belonging-to-spacexsi/) · [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/11/claude-code-settings-files-expose-thousands-of-secrets-on-github/) · [Wired](https://www.wired.com/feed/tag/ai/latest/rss)

Two separate security disclosures this week underline how much risk sits inside AI coding agents. Ars Technica reported that xAI's Grok Build coding agent leaked large volumes of confidential information, some of it belonging to SpaceX. Separately, Ars reported that Claude Code settings files have exposed thousands of secrets on GitHub, as developers commit configuration containing credentials without realizing it.

The pattern is consistent: agentic tools operate with broad filesystem, network, and shell access, and their configuration surfaces are new and poorly understood by users. Defaults that make agents convenient — persisting context, auto-loading project settings, calling external APIs — are also the defaults that leak.

The takeaway for teams deploying agents is unglamorous but urgent: treat agent config as sensitive material, scope credentials narrowly, and audit what your coding assistant writes into repositories. AI agents are becoming the softest link in the developer supply chain.
