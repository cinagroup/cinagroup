## Top 7 Stories

### 1. US Government Orders Anthropic to Suspend Claude Fable and Mythos Models

The Trump administration has ordered Anthropic to immediately suspend access to its latest Claude Fable and Mythos AI models, citing national security concerns and alleged jailbreak vulnerabilities. The Department of Commerce directive blocks both domestic and foreign access to the frontier models, marking an unprecedented intervention in the AI industry. The move has sent shockwaves through the tech sector, with the BBC, CNET, The Conversation, and dozens of outlets covering the story as it dominates Monday's news cycle.

Anthropic is now scheduled to meet with White House officials to discuss the suspension. The company has pulled the models from all access points while the review proceeds. The order raises fundamental questions about government power over AI model deployment and the precedent it sets for the industry.

### 2. Industry and Academia Rally Against Anthropic AI Restrictions

A broad coalition of cybersecurity executives, academic institutions, and industry leaders are urging the Trump administration to reverse course on the Anthropic model suspension. The Washington Post reports that cybersecurity professionals argue the restrictions hamper critical defense and research work. The Economist called the move "capricious and chaotic," while Lawfare published analysis on the legal implications of a government "kill switch" for frontier AI. European AI startups see the disruption as an opportunity to gain market share.

### 3. Anthropic Sued Over Allegedly Misleading $200/Month Claude Plan

In a separate blow, the Wall Street Journal reports that Anthropic faces a lawsuit alleging the company overpromised capabilities on its $200-per-month Claude subscription plan. The class-action suit claims users were misled about the limits and capabilities they would receive, raising questions about AI subscription business models and consumer protection in the rapidly growing AI services market.

### 4. Meta's AI Unit Described as "Soul-Crushing" — Zuckerberg Admits "Mistakes"

Multiple reports from WIRED, TechCrunch, and Futurism paint a dire picture of Meta's AI division, describing it as a "soul-crushing gulag" for engineers stuck inside. Meta CTO Andrew Bosworth publicly admitted the company's AI reorganization was "atrocious," while CEO Mark Zuckerberg acknowledged the company made "mistakes" in its AI workforce shift. The turmoil comes roughly one year after Meta tapped Alexandr Wang (Scale AI) to lead its new AI model efforts — a move that now appears to be unraveling.

### 5. Facebook Launches "AI Mode" Search Powered by Meta AI

Despite internal turmoil, Meta is pushing forward with product launches. Facebook has rolled out a new "AI Mode" search feature that pulls from public information across Meta's platforms. Analysts at Forbes estimate the feature could generate $10 billion annually for Meta. The launch comes as Google faces its own scrutiny over AI Overviews being "too chatty and personal" for a search engine, setting up a direct competition in AI-powered search.

### 6. Sarvam Becomes India's Newest AI Unicorn with $234M Round

Indian AI startup Sarvam has achieved unicorn status after closing a $234 million funding round led by HCLTech, TechCrunch reports. The raise positions Sarvam as a major player in India's growing AI ecosystem, focusing on building AI models and infrastructure tailored for Indian languages and markets. The deal signals continued strong investor appetite for AI companies outside the US-China axis.

### 7. NVIDIA Executive: AI Compute Now More Expensive Than Human Workers

In a candid assessment, an NVIDIA executive told Fortune that "the cost of compute is far beyond the costs of the employee" when it comes to current AI deployments. The statement underscores a critical tension in the AI industry: while AI capabilities continue to advance rapidly, the economic case for replacing human workers with AI remains unclear for many use cases. The comment comes as companies grapple with the real-world ROI of massive AI infrastructure investments.
