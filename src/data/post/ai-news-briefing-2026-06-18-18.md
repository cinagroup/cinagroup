## Top 7 Stories

### 1. Anthropic Files for IPO, Setting Stage for Landmark AI Public Offering

Anthropic has confidentially filed an IPO prospectus with the SEC, preparing Wall Street for what could be one of the most significant tech listings of 2026. The move comes as the AI safety-focused company accelerates its competition with OpenAI for enterprise and consumer market share.

The filing signals a new phase in the AI industry's maturation, with major players transitioning from private venture-backed operations to publicly traded companies accountable to shareholders. Anthropic's IPO is expected to value the company at a premium given its positioning in the responsible AI space and growing enterprise adoption of Claude.

### 2. Google Signs $920 Million Per Month Compute Deal with SpaceX-xAI

In a stunning move that reshapes the AI infrastructure landscape, Google will pay SpaceX $920 million per month for compute capacity at xAI data centers. The deal, reportedly worth $29.4 billion annually, comes ahead of SpaceX's anticipated IPO and represents an unprecedented level of cross-company collaboration in the AI compute space.

The agreement highlights the intensifying demand for AI training and inference capacity, with even tech giants like Google needing to secure external compute resources. The deal also raises questions about the convergence of Musk's various ventures and their competitive positioning against traditional cloud providers.

### 3. G7 Summit & VivaTech 2026: AI CEOs Meet World Leaders in France

The G7 summit in Évian, France, coinciding with VivaTech 2026, brought together heads of state with the CEOs of OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google in what CNBC called "a signal of where power sits." The unprecedented gathering focused on AI governance, export controls, and the geopolitical implications of AI advancement.

European leaders expressed growing concerns about dependence on US AI technology, with discussions centering on digital sovereignty and the need for competitive European AI capabilities. The summit also addressed AI safety standards, model access policies, and the growing rivalry with China in the AI space.

### 4. Bernie Sanders Unveils Plan for Public Ownership of AI Companies

In an AP Exclusive, Senator Bernie Sanders introduced legislation that would give the public direct ownership stakes in major AI companies. The proposal represents a dramatic shift in how society might capture the economic benefits of AI, moving beyond traditional taxation models toward direct equity participation.

The plan comes amid growing public concern about AI-driven wealth concentration and job displacement. Sanders argued that since AI systems are built on publicly funded research and societal data, the public deserves a direct financial stake in the companies commercializing these technologies.

### 5. Anthropic Employees Accuse Trump Administration of Targeting

According to The New York Times, Anthropic employees have publicly accused the Trump administration of targeting the company, marking an escalation in tensions between AI safety-focused organizations and the current administration. The allegations suggest potential regulatory or political pressure aimed at companies emphasizing AI safety guardrails.

The story highlights the increasingly political nature of AI development, with companies that advocate for safety measures finding themselves at odds with administration priorities favoring rapid deployment. The situation raises questions about the independence of AI safety research in the current political environment.

### 6. Europe's AI Sovereignty Fears Intensify at G7 and VivaTech

Reuters reports that Europe is increasingly anxious about its dependence on US AI technology, with the G7 and VivaTech events laying bare the continent's competitive disadvantages. The concerns center on both economic sovereignty and the ability to maintain independent AI governance standards.

European officials and business leaders used the France events to push for increased investment in domestic AI capabilities, with France and Germany leading calls for a coordinated European response. The discussions come as Trump's AI export policies add another layer of uncertainty for European technology planning.

### 7. Forbes Releases 2026 AI 50 List; "MANGOS" Replaces FAANG

Forbes published its annual AI 50 list highlighting the top artificial intelligence companies, while a new acronym — "MANGOS" (Meta, Apple, Nvidia, Google, OpenAI, SpaceX) — has emerged to replace the old FAANG designation, reflecting AI's dominance in reshaping the tech landscape.

The shift in terminology underscores how AI has become the central organizing principle of the tech industry, with companies evaluated primarily on their AI capabilities rather than their traditional business lines. The new roster reflects both the winners of the AI transition and the growing influence of Musk's ecosystem.
