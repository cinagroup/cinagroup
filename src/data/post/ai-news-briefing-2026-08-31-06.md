## Top 7 Stories

### 1. OpenAI Cuts Off Cursor After SpaceX Acquisition, Escalating the Musk Feud

OpenAI announced it will end model access to Cursor, the popular AI coding assistant, following its acquisition by Elon Musk's SpaceX — citing distrust of the new ownership rather than any technical issue. CNBC, Mashable, and PYMNTS report the move was framed as a contractual and trust decision, with OpenAI effectively refusing to keep supplying GPT models to a company now controlled by its most prominent rival and critic.

The fallout was immediate: Anthropic pounced, promising Cursor users higher Claude compute limits as a lifeline, per Wccftech. The episode is a rare case of a model supplier terminating a flagship customer over ownership, and it signals that frontier labs now treat control of distribution channels as strategic terrain — with the Altman-Musk rivalry making that terrain openly hostile.

### 2. Sony Music and Warner Chappell Sue Anthropic Over 'Blatant' Copyright Theft

Sony Music and Warner Chappell filed a major copyright lawsuit against Anthropic, alleging the company trained Claude on thousands of copyrighted song lyrics in a "brazen campaign" of intellectual property theft. Business Insider reports the publishers are seeking up to $150,000 per infringing song, with TechCrunch and Axios covering the scale of the alleged scraping.

The suit is the highest-profile music-industry action against an AI lab to date and lands as publishers test whether courts will treat training-data ingestion as infringement. For Anthropic, it adds legal and reputational pressure at a moment when it is also pushing into new markets — and it could set a precedent that shapes how every lab licenses creative content going forward.

### 3. Nvidia Pulls Back on AI Cloud Financing Plan as Antitrust Questions Mount

Nvidia is scaling back its AI cloud financing program amid growing antitrust scrutiny, per PYMNTS. The plan — which helped customers buy Nvidia-heavy infrastructure by offering financing tied to its own products — had drawn regulatory attention over concerns it could lock competitors out of the market.

The retreat comes as Nvidia faces heightened antitrust review of its ecosystem strategies across chips, networking, and investment. With regulators increasingly probing how the company's financial muscle reinforces its hardware dominance, the pullback is a signal that even Nvidia is now calibrating its moves to avoid a regulatory reckoning.

### 4. Anthropic Warns Hackers Are Hijacking Claude Sessions With Infostealer Malware

Anthropic issued a warning that infostealer malware is being used to hijack active Claude sessions, letting attackers drain usage quotas and take over accounts, per BleepingComputer and Search Engine Journal. The attacks target saved credentials and session tokens rather than breaking Claude's model safety itself.

The warning highlights a growing attack surface: as AI assistants become persistent, always-on work tools, session theft becomes as valuable as credential theft. It's a reminder that the enterprise AI security story is no longer just about prompt injection — it's about the same malware economy that has long plagued SaaS, now aimed at agentic platforms.

### 5. 'It Feels Like Early Covid': The Messy Scramble to Regulate AI

CNN's reporting on Washington's regulatory scramble captures an environment where AI safety risks are outpacing US rules, with agencies, states, and Congress all moving in different directions. The piece describes a fragmented landscape of executive orders, state laws, and agency guidance — a patchwork that leaves frontier labs facing inconsistent obligations.

The disarray matters because AI governance is being set by default: where regulation is slow, corporate self-governance and state-level rules fill the vacuum. As the EU and China advance more coherent national frameworks, the US approach — or lack of one — increasingly shapes where labs choose to deploy and how safety standards actually get enforced.

### 6. Google Search Is an AI-Powered Homework Helper Now

Google is deepening AI integration in Search, with Mashable reporting the service now functions as an AI-powered homework helper and PCMag noting AI Mode coverage keeps expanding despite user pushback. The features shift Search from a link directory toward a tutor-and-agent that answers, explains, and completes tasks directly.

The trajectory reinforces that Google is betting its core product on AI-generated answers, even as publishers and users complain. Every expansion moves more of the web's traffic and attention behind an AI intermediary — a dynamic that will define the economics of both Google's ad business and the open web for the foreseeable future.

### 7. Meta Expands AI Tools for Small Businesses

Meta is rolling out an expanded suite of AI tools for small businesses, adding automation and AI-assisted features designed to help SMBs manage ads, customer service, and content, per Portal ERP. The push extends Meta's earlier AI-for-business efforts deeper into the long tail of advertisers who are its most reliable revenue base.

For Meta, the move is defensive and offensive at once: AI tools make its ad platform stickier against rivals while automating away the complexity that small advertisers struggle with. It also puts Meta in direct competition with AI-native marketing startups — and signals that the platform layer, not just the model layer, is where AI monetization is now happening.
