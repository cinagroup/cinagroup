## Top 7 Stories

### 1. OpenAI Cuts Off AI Models for SpaceX-Owned Cursor, Escalating Feud with Musk

OpenAI is cutting off access to its AI models for Cursor, the coding assistant acquired by SpaceX earlier this year, according to Reuters. The move dramatically escalates the feud between Sam Altman and Elon Musk, turning a business dispute into an active denial of service against a flagship developer tool used by millions of engineers.

The decision has immediate practical consequences: Cursor users who relied on OpenAI models will need to switch to alternatives, and the episode shows how ownership changes can ripple through the AI stack overnight. It also signals that OpenAI is willing to weaponize model access as leverage in its rivalry with Musk, raising questions about how much of the AI supply chain now hinges on personal conflicts between a handful of billionaires.

### 2. NVIDIA Reports Record Second-Quarter Results as AI Spending Surges

NVIDIA announced financial results for its second quarter of fiscal 2027, delivering another record quarter as AI infrastructure spending shows no sign of slowing. Axios summed up the mood with its "Nvidia almighty" headline, noting that chip riches continue to flood through the AI universe even as some investors publicly debate whether a bubble is forming.

The results reinforce NVIDIA's position as the indispensable supplier of the AI buildout. But the surrounding chatter — from bubble warnings in Foreign Policy to comparisons with Apple's historical valuation in Barron's — suggests the market is increasingly parsing NVIDIA's results for signs of demand durability, not just growth.

### 3. Anthropic Previews the Model Hardware Standard

Anthropic published a preview of its "Model Hardware Standard," an initiative aimed at standardizing the hardware interfaces that AI models rely on. The announcement positions Anthropic as a shaper of the physical infrastructure layer of AI, not just the model layer.

If the standard gains traction, it could reduce the fragmentation that plagues AI hardware today and make it easier for models to run efficiently across different chips and devices. The move also deepens Anthropic's push into the ecosystem side of AI, competing with NVIDIA and others for influence over how the hardware-software stack evolves.

### 4. OpenAI Previews GPT-5.6 Sol, a Next-Generation Model

OpenAI previewed GPT-5.6 Sol, describing it as a next-generation model in the GPT-5 line. The release continues OpenAI's rapid cadence of model updates, with Sol positioned as a significant step forward in capability and efficiency.

The preview comes amid a broader flurry of OpenAI announcements, including early results for its "Jalapeño" inference engine, which the company says shows industry-leading speed and efficiency. Together, the releases suggest OpenAI is racing to defend its frontier position on multiple fronts: raw capability, inference cost, and developer mindshare — the latter now complicated by the Cursor cutoff.

### 5. AWS and NVIDIA to Deliver Two Million Additional GPUs for Agentic and Physical AI

AWS and NVIDIA announced plans to deliver two million additional GPUs along with next-generation infrastructure purpose-built for agentic and physical AI workloads. The deal cements the hyperscaler-chipmaker alliance at the center of the AI buildout and signals that enterprises are still nowhere near done buying compute.

The emphasis on agentic and physical AI is notable: both companies are betting that the next wave of demand will come not from chatbots but from autonomous agents and robotics that consume far more inference compute. If that bet is right, today's GPU buildout is merely the foundation for an even larger one.

### 6. Meta Projected It Could Spend $10 Billion on Anthropic's AI

The New York Times reported that Meta has projected it could spend up to $10 billion on Anthropic's AI. The figure, reportedly weighing a major commercial relationship with the frontier lab, underscores how even the biggest model developers are now hedging by buying access to rivals' technology.

For Anthropic, a deal of that scale would be transformative, rivaling the Microsoft-OpenAI relationship in size. For Meta, it reflects an uncomfortable strategic reality: despite billions spent on its own AI research, the company may still need frontier models from elsewhere — a dynamic that will shape competitive positioning for years.

### 7. Jensen Huang Takes Trump's Call Mid-All-Hands — and Says AGI Is 'Senseless'

WIRED reported that Jensen Huang took a call from Donald Trump in the middle of an NVIDIA all-hands meeting, while Mashable covered Huang's blunt remarks that AGI is "already here" and that the milestone itself is "senseless." The two stories capture NVIDIA's twin realities: deep entanglement with Washington on chips policy, and a CEO who dismisses the industry's favorite goalpost.

Huang's framing reframes the AI debate away from a single AGI moment and toward continuous capability growth. Meanwhile, his direct line to the White House matters as the Trump administration weighs tariffs and export controls on chips — policies the AI industry has reportedly called "the single dumbest way imaginable" to tax semiconductors.
