## Top 7 Stories

**1. Nvidia Scales Back Its $250 Billion Guarantee for OpenAI's Ohio Data Center**

The Wall Street Journal reports exclusively that Nvidia has downsized its plans to guarantee up to $250 billion in credit for OpenAI's Ohio data center, with reports putting the revised backstop at below $120 billion. Reuters and Investing.com confirm the reduction, which comes as OpenAI's buildout plans shift and the company prepares for its IPO.

The move is significant because the guarantee was one of the largest financial commitments in AI infrastructure history — a signal of how deeply Nvidia and OpenAI are financially intertwined. Scaling it back suggests both parties are re-pricing risk: OpenAI's construction timeline is evolving, and Nvidia is balancing its financing exposure against a $500 billion infrastructure push of its own, reportedly mobilized with Goldman Sachs.

**2. Alibaba's Qwen AI Models Hit 3 Billion Downloads, Passing Meta and Google**

Bloomberg reports that Alibaba's Qwen family of AI models has surpassed 3 billion downloads, overtaking Meta and Google on the download charts. The milestone cements Qwen as the most-downloaded open-weight model series, with Business Standard and BusinessLine echoing the figure.

The numbers matter because they show open-source AI is increasingly a Chinese-led race: Alibaba's aggressive open-weight strategy — and China's Z.ai reportedly nearing Anthropic's Mythos 5 in cyber-defense tests — is giving global developers an alternative to US frontier labs. For Meta and Google, it raises the stakes on their own open-model bets, since ecosystem share among developers increasingly determines which models become default infrastructure.

**3. Anthropic's Revenue Surges Past $11.5 Billion in the Second Quarter**

Bloomberg reports that Anthropic's revenue surged to over $11.5 billion in the second quarter, while Benzinga cites reports projecting the company could reach $200 billion in revenue by 2028. The figures mark a dramatic acceleration for the lab as it positions itself ahead of a widely anticipated IPO.

The trajectory makes Anthropic's reported $2 trillion IPO target look less like a fantasy and more like a benchmark the market is starting to price. But it also sharpens the pressure on the company's restraint-first stance — including holding back its stronger "Model 2" over safety concerns — as investors weigh growth against a lab that has repeatedly signaled it will prioritize alignment over release cadence.

**4. Anthropic Rolls Out Text Watermarking for Claude, Drawing User Backlash**

Anthropic has begun watermarking Claude's AI-generated text, with Mashable, BleepingComputer, and Search Engine Journal covering how the feature works — subtle statistical patterns embedded in output that can mark content as machine-written. Business Insider reports some Claude users are canceling subscriptions over the feature, and Anthropic has publicly defended and explained its approach.

The rollout is a watershed for AI provenance: text watermarking has long been considered technically harder than image or video watermarking, and Claude is one of the first frontier assistants to ship it by default. The backlash illustrates the tension ahead — users want privacy and flexibility, while labs, publishers, and regulators increasingly want verifiable proof of AI authorship. The key question is whether Anthropic's detection method holds up as users and competitors probe for ways to defeat it.

**5. Nvidia Discloses $21 Billion SpaceX Stake in Second-Quarter Filing**

CNBC and Bloomberg report that Nvidia's second-quarter 13F filing discloses a $21 billion stake in SpaceX — roughly 123 million shares, making Nvidia the rocket company's sixth-largest shareholder — plus $30 billion in Intel shares. SpaceX becomes Nvidia's second-largest holding.

The disclosure reframes Nvidia as more than a chipmaker: it is becoming a strategic investor across the AI-adjacent economy, from launch infrastructure to rival silicon. For markets, the filing also feeds the narrative that Nvidia's balance sheet — and its $500 billion AI financing platform — is being deployed to make the AI buildout "too big to fail," as SiliconANGLE put it.

**6. Google Launches Gemini 3.7 Flash in AI Mode, Courting Developers With Lower Pricing**

Google is rolling out Gemini 3.7 Flash, bringing the model to AI Mode in Search, according to Search Engine Journal, with AI Business reporting a lower introductory price aimed at attracting developers. The launch comes amid a reported AI leadership shakeup and a DeepMind exodus that has analysts asking whether Google can sustain its Gemini push.

The strategic logic is clear: Google is betting that cheaper, faster inference wins developer mindshare at a moment when OpenAI and Google are both "selling faster AI" as the product itself. But the timing — with reports that Sergey Brin is pushing Google to go all in on Gemini even as key DeepMind researchers depart — makes the release as much a test of organizational stability as of model quality.

**7. OpenAI Upheaval Mounts as Sam Altman Readies the IPO Push**

The Financial Times reports that upheaval at OpenAI is mounting just as Sam Altman prepares the company's IPO push. The report follows a wave of executive departures that CNBC flagged as a "huge red flag," and comes alongside OpenAI's reported $6.5 billion bet on Jony Ive's hardware venture and its new AI smart speaker plans.

The timing is delicate: OpenAI wants to go public at a moment of maximum momentum, but churn at the top is exactly what IPO investors scrutinize. With Anthropic's revenue surge and its own $2 trillion IPO ambitions looming, OpenAI must show that its leadership team — not just its models — can hold together through the most consequential public debut in AI history.
