## Top 7 Stories

### 1. Anthropic Files for IPO, Leading the Race to Go Public

Anthropic has officially filed to go public, becoming the first of the trillion-dollar AI startups to take this step. The filing, reported by Reuters, The New York Times, and NBC News on June 1-2, sets the stage for what could be one of the most anticipated tech IPOs in history. The move comes as Anthropic's Claude AI platform has gained significant enterprise traction, with revenue growth accelerating in recent quarters.

The filing intensifies the competitive race with OpenAI, which is also reportedly preparing for a public offering. Market analysts suggest Anthropic's earlier filing could give it a first-mover advantage in capturing investor appetite for pure-play AI companies, especially as public markets have shown strong enthusiasm for AI-related stocks in 2026.

### 2. Trump Signs AI Executive Order Targeting Frontier Models

President Trump has signed a previously shelved executive order focused on AI innovation and security, with the White House issuing new directives specifically targeting frontier AI models. The order, signed on June 2, outlines new regulatory frameworks for the development and deployment of the most powerful AI systems.

CISA director Brandon Andersen announced that implementing directives for the executive order will be released this week. The order is expected to have significant implications for AI developers, government contractors, and critical infrastructure operators, creating new compliance requirements while also promoting AI innovation in strategic sectors.

### 3. Microsoft Unveils New AI Models to Reduce OpenAI Dependence

Microsoft has announced a new family of proprietary AI models designed to reduce its reliance on OpenAI's technology and lower costs for developers. The announcement, reported by CNBC on June 2, signals a strategic shift in Microsoft's AI strategy as the company seeks greater independence in its AI stack.

The new models are expected to power various Microsoft products, including Copilot and Azure AI services. This development could reshape the competitive landscape among hyperscalers, as Microsoft positions itself with independent AI capabilities while maintaining its partnership with OpenAI.

### 4. NVIDIA Corners the AI Agent Stack with Vera CPU

NVIDIA is making a major push into the agentic AI space with its Vera CPU architecture, purpose-built for AI agents rather than human users. CEO Jensen Huang stated that Vera opens "a market that never existed before," positioning the chip as the foundational processor for autonomous AI systems.

The announcement, covered by digitimes and The Rundown AI, highlights NVIDIA's strategy to dominate the entire AI agent technology stack — from training GPUs to inference CPUs. Vera represents NVIDIA's bet that the future of computing will be driven by autonomous agents running continuously on specialized hardware, creating a new category of computing workload.

### 5. AI Companies Barreling Toward Huge Wall Street Debuts

A wave of AI companies is preparing for major Wall Street debuts in 2026, with U.S. News & World Report highlighting the biggest players expected to go public. Beyond Anthropic, companies including Databricks, Canva, and others are reportedly lining up for IPOs, capitalizing on the current market enthusiasm for AI stocks.

The timing reflects a confluence of factors: strong AI revenue growth, favorable market conditions for tech IPOs, and increasing pressure from private investors for liquidity. Wall Street has already responded positively, with AI stocks pushing major indices to new records in early June.

### 6. China's DeepSeek Set to Join AI Fundraising Frenzy

China's DeepSeek is preparing to join the global AI fundraising frenzy, Bloomberg reported on June 4. The Chinese AI startup, known for its cost-effective approach to AI model development, is expected to raise significant capital as it scales operations and competes with Western counterparts.

DeepSeek's entry into the global fundraising landscape underscores the increasingly international nature of the AI race. The company has gained attention for demonstrating that competitive AI models can be built at a fraction of the cost of U.S. rivals, potentially disrupting the capital-intensive model that has dominated the industry.

### 7. Wall Street Hits New Records on AI Stock Boom

Wall Street indices inched to new records in early June, driven largely by booming AI stock valuations. Spectrum News reported on June 2 that AI-related equities continue to outperform broader market indices, reflecting sustained investor confidence in the sector's growth trajectory.

The rally has been broad-based, encompassing chipmakers like NVIDIA, cloud providers like Microsoft and Google parent Alphabet, and emerging AI pure-play companies. Analysts note that the current AI-driven market expansion mirrors the internet boom of the late 1990s, though with more established revenue fundamentals underlying the valuations.
