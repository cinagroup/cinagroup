### 1. OpenAI Revenue Tops $10 Billion in a Quarter as Losses Widen

OpenAI crossed $10 billion in quarterly revenue for the first time, a milestone that underscores how quickly enterprise and consumer adoption of its models has scaled. Reporting pegged losses at roughly $10.9 billion for the period, with a loss-per-dollar-of-revenue figure around $1.09.

The numbers frame the central tension in the current AI buildout: revenue growth is extraordinary, but so is the cost of compute, talent and infrastructure required to sustain it. Analysts noted that the loss ratio is improving even as absolute losses rise.

For the broader market, OpenAI's disclosure sets a benchmark against which rivals' enterprise traction will be judged — and a reminder that frontier AI remains a capital-intensive business even for its clear leader.

The competitive logic is straightforward: whoever owns the document, spreadsheet and email surface controls the highest-frequency distribution channel for AI assistance. Anthropic is now competing there directly.

### 3. Anthropic Brings Claude Into Google Meet for Live Task Handling

Sources: [Reuters via MSN](https://www.msn.com/en-au/technology/tech-companies/anthropic-brings-claude-to-google-meet-to-tackle-tasks-live/ar-AA2hae6h?ocid=BingNewsVerp) · [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1030005/anthropic-claude-fable-mythos-5-2)

Anthropic integrated Claude into Google Meet, allowing the assistant to handle tasks live during meetings rather than only after the fact. The move extends Claude from a chat window into the real-time collaboration layer where much enterprise work actually happens.

The integration is part of a broader Anthropic model rollout, with the company shipping new Claude variants aimed at different workloads and price points.

Meeting assistants are becoming a key battleground for AI agents, since they combine transcription, summarization and action-taking — the exact combination that turns a passive assistant into an active one.

Google launched a healthcare-focused Gemini offering aimed at the industry's persistent data and documentation problem — administrative paperwork, records handling and clinical workflow friction. Health systems including Northwell were cited as early partners hoping the tool reduces costs passed on to patients.

The pitch is less about diagnosis and more about back-office burden, where hospitals spend heavily on manual documentation and data reconciliation.

Healthcare is one of the highest-value and most heavily regulated verticals for AI, so Google's entry tests whether frontier models can deliver efficiency gains without tripping compliance and safety concerns.
