## Top 7 Stories

### 1. Meta acquires AI coding startup Conductor

Sources: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/04/meta-acquires-ai-coding-startup-conductor/) · [Axios](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/exclusive-meta-buys-ai-startup-181505877.html)

Meta has acquired Conductor, a startup building AI-assisted software development tooling, according to reports from TechCrunch and Axios. The deal puts Meta into a crowded race among hyperscalers to own the developer coding workflow — a segment where Anthropic's Claude Code, OpenAI's Codex tooling, and GitHub Copilot have already staked out ground.

The acquisition signals Meta's intent to compete on developer productivity rather than only on consumer chat assistants and open-weight models. Axios characterized the move as Meta joining "the race to own coding," suggesting the deal is strategic rather than a pure acquihire.

Terms were not disclosed in the reporting. Expect integration questions to follow — chief among them whether Conductor's tooling folds into Meta's internal developer stack or ships as a commercial product.

### 2. Anthropic's amended IPO filing: $33B revenue, $104B in losses

Sources: [The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/02/business/anthropic-earnings-ai.html) · [The Baltimore Sun](https://www.baltimoresun.com/2026/11/02/anthropic-revenue-fresh-ipo-filing/) · [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/anthropic-amended-ipo-filing-5-080000335.html)

Anthropic updated its IPO filing with third-quarter numbers that show explosive revenue growth paired with equally dramatic losses. The company reported quarterly revenue around $33 billion, while cumulative 2026 losses reached roughly $104 billion ahead of its planned Nasdaq debut.

The figures, first surfaced in an amended S-1 and covered by the NYT and other outlets, make Anthropic one of the fastest-growing enterprise software businesses ever to approach public markets — and one of the most cash-hungry. Analysts have zeroed in on the gap between gross revenue and compute costs as the central question for prospective investors.

The filing sets up a landmark listing for the AI sector. Whether public markets will price AI labs on growth or on path-to-profitability is now the defining open question for the whole industry.

### 3. Google will cut off school search access if districts reject its AI tools

Sources: [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/11/google-will-let-schools-skip-ai-tools-but-they-have-to-give-up-search-too/) · [CBS News](https://www.msn.com/en-us/news/other/google-to-end-search-access-for-schools-that-block-its-ai-tools/ar-AA2gGdEW?ocid=BingNewsVerp) · [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/04/duckduckgo-pitches-itself-to-schools-amid-backlash-over-googles-ai-policy/)

Google is telling school districts that declining its AI classroom tools also means losing Google Search access for students, a linkage that has drawn accusations of coercion. Ars Technica reported that the policy was framed as a bundled agreement, not a menu of independent choices.

The backlash was immediate. New York Mayor Zohran Mamdani criticized the company publicly, and DuckDuckGo began pitching itself to schools as an alternative — a rare opening for a Google competitor in the education market. Google has since pushed back, insisting the arrangement is not "extortion."

The episode illustrates a broader tension: as AI features get woven into core products, the line between an upsell and a precondition for access becomes politically radioactive, especially where children and public institutions are involved.

### 4. OpenAI is already courting 2028 presidential candidates

Sources: [Politico](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/11/02/openai-is-already-pitching-2028-hopefuls-on-how-to-talk-about-ai-01144821) · [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/04/openai-is-already-trying-to-shape-the-2028-election/) · [Unite.AI](https://www.unite.ai/openais-lehane-calls-ai-the-number-one-issue-of-the-2028-election/)

OpenAI is already briefing prospective 2028 presidential candidates on how to talk about artificial intelligence, with a company lobbyist calling AI "the number-one issue" of the next presidential race. Politico first reported the outreach; TechCrunch followed with analysis of the strategy.

The move comes days after the 2026 midterms and marks an unusually early entry into presidential-cycle politicking for an AI lab. OpenAI's argument, per the reporting, is that candidates need a coherent AI message regardless of party — and that the company is well positioned to supply one.

Watch for whether rival labs mirror the playbook. Anthropic, Google, and Meta all have Washington operations, and an early lobbying edge on the defining technology issue of the cycle could prove durable.

### 5. AI PACs spent millions on the midterms — with mixed returns

Sources: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/04/ai-pacs-spent-millions-trying-to-shape-congress-heres-how-they-fared/) · [The Intercept](https://theintercept.com/2026/10/30/aipac-ai-spending-midterm-election/) · [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/11/big-tech-claims-voters-ai-backlash-is-anti-trump-not-anti-tech/)

AI-aligned political action committees poured millions into 2026 congressional races, and the results were uneven. TechCrunch's post-election accounting found that several high-profile AI-backed candidates won while others lost despite heavy spending, suggesting the money bought influence but not a reliable electoral machine.

The Intercept's pre-election tally documented the scale of the spending, while Ars Technica reported that Big Tech is now arguing voters' AI backlash is really an "anti-Trump" sentiment rather than "anti-tech" — a framing that lets the industry avoid treating public skepticism about AI as a structural problem.

The takeaway for the industry: AI money is now a real force in elections, but it has not yet bought a coherent political coalition. That gap is likely to be tested again in 2028.

### 6. Nvidia tightens controls on China's access to US AI chips

Sources: [Independent Journal Review](https://ijr.com/discover/article/nvidia-china-chips-data-centers-artificial-intelligence-models-3b6e91f5-66f0-4cd7-a66f-9c30de33da8c) · [24/7 Wall St.](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/11/03/nvidia-rises-3-despite-ai-ceos-warning-on-chinese-chip-competition-amd-climbs-5-broadcom-gains-3/)

Nvidia is moving to close a backdoor route by which Chinese buyers have been obtaining advanced US AI chips, reportedly through an unwritten policy applied to data-center customers. The company is also publicly defending itself after a spy investigation report, with CEO Jensen Huang addressing the allegations directly.

The enforcement push coincides with Huang's own warning about Chinese chip competition — an acknowledgment that export controls are accelerating domestic alternatives. Despite the warnings, Nvidia stock rose about 3% while AMD climbed 5% and Broadcom gained 3%, indicating investors see the restrictions as manageable.

The bigger story is structural: the US-China chip split is hardening, and every tightening of controls gives Chinese chipmakers a stronger domestic mandate.

### 7. Almost nobody uses AI agents to order food, Uber and DoorDash admit

Sources: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/04/almost-no-one-uses-ai-agents-to-book-their-uber-company-says/) · [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/10/doordash-says-outside-ai-agents-have-only-led-to-hundreds-of-orders-so-far/)

Uber says almost no one uses AI agents to book rides, and DoorDash reports that outside AI agents have driven only hundreds of orders — a rounding error against its overall volume. Both disclosures landed within days of each other and cut against the narrative that agentic commerce is already mainstream.

The numbers are a reality check for the agent economy. While labs and platform vendors demo agents that browse, book, and buy, consumers have largely not adopted the behavior — friction, trust, and unclear value remain barriers.

DoorDash is hedging by launching its own text-to-order AI agent inside messaging apps, betting that a first-party agent with native integration can do what third-party agents have not.
