## Top 7 Stories

### 1. OpenAI raises $11 billion at a $1.25 trillion valuation, below its original target
Sources: [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/openai-secures-11-billion-funding-125-trillion-valuation-bloomberg-news-reports-2026-11-06/) · [TechCrunch via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/openai-reportedly-raises-additional-11-225346372.html) · [24/7 Wall St.](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/11/07/openai-just-raised-11-billion-at-a-1-25-trillion-valuation-below-the-1-4-trillion-it-first-sought/)

OpenAI has closed an $11 billion funding round at a valuation of $1.25 trillion, according to reporting originally broken by Bloomberg and confirmed by Reuters. The figure lands below the roughly $1.4 trillion valuation the company had initially sought, a sign that investor appetite for frontier-lab equity has cooled somewhat from the peak of the AI boom.

The raise arrives in a markedly different market environment than OpenAI's earlier rounds, with public tech stocks selling off sharply around the US midterm elections and voter pushback against AI data-center buildouts dominating headlines. Coverage framed the discount as a modest but meaningful recalibration of the largest private valuations in tech.

OpenAI has not issued a detailed public statement on the terms of the round. Observers are watching whether the smaller-than-hoped valuation sets a new benchmark for the broader cohort of AI companies seeking capital or listing shares in the coming quarters.

### 2. Anthropic's IPO valuation slides as AI trade and data-center backlash bite
Sources: [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/07/anthropic-ipo-ai-data-center-backlash.html) · [Axios via MSN](https://www.msn.com/en-us/money/general/anthropic-s-falling-ipo-valuation-raises-stakes-for-broader-ai-trade/ar-AA2gV6qT?ocid=BingNewsVerp) · [The Telegraph via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/anthropic-ipo-turmoil-ai-sell-113638328.html)

Anthropic's planned listing — widely billed as one of the largest IPOs on record — ran into turbulence this week as a broad AI sell-off and post-midterm backlash against data centers pulled its expected valuation lower. CNBC reported that the IPO now meets an "AI backlash" moment after midterm results were read as a rebuke of aggressive data-center expansion.

Axios noted that Anthropic's falling IPO valuation raises the stakes for the broader AI trade, since the company's pricing is treated as a bellwether for how public markets value frontier-model businesses. The Telegraph reported the listing is in "turmoil" as the sell-off hammers valuations across the sector.

Earlier filings showed Anthropic's growth slowing and costs rising in the third quarter, adding fundamental pressure on top of the macro picture. How the deal prices will be closely read as a signal for other AI companies weighing public listings.

### 3. Google's school AI policy backfires — schools told to accept AI or risk losing Search
Sources: [TechCrunch via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/duckduckgo-pitches-itself-schools-amid-192952217.html) · [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/google-tries-to-walk-back-controversial-school-ai-policy-following-political-backlash-2000834986) · [Staten Island Advance](https://www.silive.com/news/2026/11/google-told-nyc-schools-to-accept-ai-or-lose-access-to-google-search-report-says.html)

Google found itself on the defensive over a policy that, as reported, told school districts they could opt out of its AI features but might then lose access to Google Search. The framing drew immediate political criticism, and Gizmodo reported the company attempting to walk the policy back following the backlash.

The story landed hardest in New York City, where the Staten Island Advance reported that Google told NYC schools to accept AI or lose access to Google Search. The episode became a flashpoint in the wider debate over bundling AI features into core productivity and search products used by public institutions.

Rivals moved quickly to exploit the opening. TechCrunch reported that DuckDuckGo pitched itself directly to schools amid the controversy, positioning privacy-focused search as an alternative for districts uneasy about the bundling.

### 4. Meta raises $15 billion in a five-part bond sale to fund AI spending
Sources: [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/technology/meta-raises-15-billion-in-five-part-us-bond-sale-2026-11-03/) · [Bloomberg via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/articles/meta-bond-sale-shows-reality-200000968.html) · [24/7 Wall St. via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/meta-just-paid-7-5-131327635.html)

Meta raised $15 billion through a five-part US bond sale, according to Reuters, as the company leans on debt markets to sustain its enormous AI capital-expenditure program. The deal drew roughly $58 billion in orders, underscoring still-strong demand for hyperscaler credit even amid wider concerns about AI spending.

The pricing was not cheap: 24/7 Wall St. reported Meta paid about 7.5% to borrow for 30 years. Bloomberg framed the sale as evidence of a "new reality for tech debt," in which the largest AI spenders increasingly resemble capital-intensive industrial borrowers rather than cash-rich software companies.

The bond sale comes as investors scrutinize whether AI infrastructure spending will generate returns commensurate with its scale. Meta has not signaled any pullback in its data-center and compute commitments.

### 5. NVIDIA H200 chips pile up in Hong Kong as Beijing withholds import approval
Sources: [Reuters via MSN](https://www.msn.com/en-us/money/general/exclusive-nvidia-chips-pile-up-in-hong-kong-as-china-withholds-import-approval/ar-AA2gOoxt?ocid=BingNewsVerp) · [DatacenterDynamics](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/almost-600000-nvidia-h200s-stranded-in-hong-kong-as-chinese-govt-stalls-on-import-approvals-report/) · [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/nvidia-h200-chips-hong-kong-china-import-approval)

Reuters reported exclusively that NVIDIA H200 accelerators are piling up in Hong Kong after Chinese authorities withheld import approvals for the chips. DatacenterDynamics put the scale of the backlog at almost 600,000 H200 units stranded in the territory.

The holdup is widely read as a deliberate lever to steer Chinese buyers toward domestic AI silicon vendors. The Next Web reported that Beijing is holding back approvals in a push to boost indigenous chip capacity, leaving exporters and distributors with inventory stuck in limbo.

The standoff complicates NVIDIA's China business, which has been repeatedly reshaped by export controls and shifting approval regimes. Neither NVIDIA nor Chinese regulators issued detailed public comment on the specific approval pause.

Google researchers bound by non-disclosure agreements are pressing for the freedom to publicly discuss AI risks, according to The Washington Post. The internal push reflects growing friction between frontier-lab secrecy norms and researchers' sense that safety-relevant knowledge is being kept from public scrutiny.

Yahoo Tech reported that NDA-bound researchers want latitude to speak publicly about the risks they study, framing it as a matter of scientific openness and accountability at a moment when AI capabilities are advancing quickly.

The demand lands amid a broader debate over transparency at the largest AI labs, where safety research often sits behind strict confidentiality. Google has not publicly detailed any change to its policies in response.

The question of development speed has become central to the AI policy debate, particularly with Anthropic in the middle of an IPO process that puts unusual public scrutiny on its safety posture and growth trajectory.
