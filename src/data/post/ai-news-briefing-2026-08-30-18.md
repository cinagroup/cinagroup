## Top 7 Stories

### 1. Nvidia Insists It Can Keep 'Printing Money' to Fund the AI Boom

The Wall Street Journal reports Nvidia's leadership argues the company can sustain its extraordinary cash generation to bankroll the AI buildout, even as questions mount about concentration risk in the sector. The confidence comes as Nvidia reportedly weighs expanding beyond chips: it is in talks to invest in Perplexity AI at a $30 billion valuation and has been linked to a roughly $13 billion investment in Hugging Face, per semivision and multiple outlets.

The story matters because it reframes Nvidia from a pure silicon vendor into a financier and consolidator of the AI application layer. If Nvidia starts writing big checks into model hubs and consumer AI, it gains a hedge against any future chip-cycle slowdown — and a seat at more tables of the AI economy.

### 2. Anthropic Previews a Standard for AI Agents in the Physical World

Anthropic opened a research preview of the Model Hardware Standard (MHS), a shared specification for AI agents to safely operate physical devices — from lab instruments to factory machinery — per CNBC, Reuters, and Ars Technica. It marks Anthropic's first formal move into physical AI, with WIRED and Fortune noting the target users are scientists and manufacturers who want to "bring equipment to life" through Claude-driven agents.

MHS is significant because hardware fragmentation is one of the biggest blockers to agentic robotics: every device speaks a different protocol. A common standard could do for physical AI what USB did for peripherals, and Anthropic's early move positions it to shape safety and interoperability norms before rivals lock in their own.

### 3. Google Expands AI Mode in Search — Whether Users Like It or Not

Google is pushing AI-generated answers deeper into Search, with PCMag reporting the company is expanding AI Mode coverage even as some users push back. The shift extends to commerce: Google's AI Mode added a Choice Hotels booking option, letting agents complete transactions directly inside search results, per Hotel News Resource.

The trajectory is unmistakable — search is becoming an agentic commerce layer rather than a list of links. That raises the stakes for Google's advertising business and for regulators, since AI Mode now sits between users and the open web, deciding both what users see and where their money goes.

### 4. South Korea's 'AI for All' Gives Every Citizen Free AI Access

The Wall Street Journal reports South Korea is launching an "AI for All" program that gives every citizen free access to frontier AI with no token limits, tapping domestic carriers and platforms including SK Telecom, Kakao, and KT to deliver the service. Analysts see the program as a national-strategy play to reduce dependence on US AI providers.

South Korea is effectively treating AI like a public utility — an approach no other major economy has attempted at this scale. If it works, it could become a template for other governments weighing how to guarantee AI access, and it puts pressure on US labs to compete on price and sovereignty terms in Asian markets.

### 5. Workday's Earnings Show AI Agents Now Drive 25% of New Revenue

Workday beat Q2 estimates as AI agent adoption surged, with AI-related annual recurring revenue jumping 200% to nearly $600 million and AI contributing roughly 25% of new revenue, per WSJ and SiliconANGLE. The company raised its FY2027 guidance on the strength of enterprise demand for its agents.

The results offer some of the clearest public evidence yet that enterprises are paying real money for agentic AI — not just experimenting with it. For the broader market, Workday's numbers are a useful reality check on the "agents are coming" narrative: the revenue is arriving now, and it is showing up in core software categories like HR and finance.

### 6. OpenAI and Anthropic Battle Big Tech for AI Talent

Business Insider surveyed tech workers on the intensifying fight for AI talent between frontier labs and Big Tech, asking who is winning the loyalty war. The piece captures a market where OpenAI and Anthropic are offering massive compensation and mission-driven work, while Google, Meta, and Microsoft counter with stability, scale, and research freedom.

The talent war matters because model quality is still largely a function of who can assemble the best research teams — and poaching cascades shift the frontier. With both labs growing headcount aggressively while platforms retaliate with retention packages, expect compensation inflation to keep rising and more high-profile defections before year-end.

### 7. Jensen Huang: AGI Is Already Here — and the Milestone Is 'Senseless'

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang said AGI is effectively already here and called the milestone "senseless," per Mashable, arguing the debate over when AGI arrives is a distraction from the real work of deploying AI at scale. The comments echo his recent emphasis on AI as an infrastructure buildout rather than a single breakthrough event.

Huang's framing is notable because he is the industry's most-watched executive on AI capex. By declaring the AGI debate settled, he redirects attention to the things Nvidia sells — compute, networking, and full-stack systems — and implicitly challenges the narrative that the industry is waiting on a research breakthrough that could upend the current buildout.
