## Top 7 Stories

### 1. Meta AI glasses privacy backlash grows as covert recordings spread
Ars Technica reports that as demand for Meta AI glasses explodes, it's becoming harder to avoid being recorded — and CBS News highlights the case of a woman covertly filmed by a Meta AI smart glasses wearer, who said "I had no say" in being captured. The glasses' discreet form factor makes consent nearly impossible to establish, and the stories are fueling a broader public reckoning with always-on wearable cameras.

The episode lands as Meta pushes Ray-Ban and Oakley AI glasses into the mainstream. Privacy advocates argue the burden is on Meta to design in visible recording indicators and stronger consent defaults before the devices become ubiquitous — a debate that could shape wearable AI regulation.

### 2. OpenAI publicly calls for stronger AI laws in California
Politico reports OpenAI is urging California lawmakers to pass stronger AI regulation, an unusual public posture for the frontier lab. The push marks a shift from defensive lobbying toward active advocacy for state-level rules, and comes as California weighs omnibus AI legislation covering safety, transparency, and liability.

The move is notable both substantively and strategically: OpenAI is positioning itself as a constructive player in the policy debate while competitors and critics alike question how far the company will actually go. California's choices often become de facto national standards, so OpenAI's endorsement could meaningfully shape what gets enacted.

### 3. Anthropic IPO filing will show AI backlash as a risk factor, sources say
CNBC reports, citing sources, that Anthropic's forthcoming IPO filing will list the AI backlash as a risk factor, acknowledging public skepticism and regulatory uncertainty as material threats to its business. The disclosure would put a mainstream-IPO stamp on concerns that have been circulating in policy circles for years.

With a record-shattering IPO reportedly in the works, the filing will be scrutinized not just for financials but for how the company frames the structural risks of the AI boom — from data disputes to reputational swings. How Anthropic prices those risks could influence how the entire sector is valued.

### 4. Nvidia's AVO hits 100% on ARC-AGI-3, spotlighting agent harnesses over raw models
NVIDIA announced its AVO (agentic vision/architecture) system reached 100% on ARC-AGI-3, which the company describes as a frontier-level general-purpose architecture for long-horizon autonomous agents. TechCrunch's coverage frames the result as evidence that "the harness, not the AI model, is now the real hero," while VentureBeat reports Nvidia found simple linear math can replace costly AI model handoffs in agent pipelines.

The milestone feeds a fast-growing industry thesis: agent orchestration, memory, and control layers matter as much as — or more than — raw model intelligence. If control infrastructure is where the value is shifting, expect agent frameworks, evaluation suites, and safety tooling to become the next battleground.

### 5. Nvidia takes a stake in data center developer Cloverleaf
TechCrunch reports Nvidia has partnered with data center developer Cloverleaf, with Dealroom adding that the chip giant took a minority stake in the power-focused firm to fuel AI data centers. It's the latest in Nvidia's string of infrastructure investments aimed at easing the power bottleneck that now constrains AI buildout as much as chip supply.

By investing in the sites that will host its GPUs, Nvidia is vertically integrating around its own demand — and hedging against data center power and permitting delays that could slow the AI capex cycle it depends on. Watch for more energy and site-development deals ahead of its earnings.

### 6. FTC urged to investigate AI firms for destroying books
Axios reports exclusively that advocates are urging the FTC to investigate AI companies for hoarding and destroying millions of books, with The Register and CBS News picking up the story. The complaints allege that firms acquired physical book collections for training data and then destroyed them, raising copyright and evidence-preservation questions.

The campaign targets the rawest edge of the AI training-data debate: what happens to the physical works consumed in the race for corpus. An FTC probe would escalate the copyright fight from civil litigation into federal enforcement, with potential implications for how training data is acquired and documented industry-wide.

### 7. Anthropic hires ex-Google chip chief as it pushes into hardware
Bloomberg reports Anthropic has hired a former Google chip chief as the AI lab pushes into hardware. The hire signals Anthropic is moving beyond model development toward owning more of its silicon and infrastructure stack — a pattern already established by OpenAI and increasingly common among frontier labs.

Hardware ownership gives labs leverage over cost, supply, and performance tuning, but it also brings enormous capital demands. For a company reportedly approaching an IPO, the timing suggests Anthropic wants to show investors it can control its own compute destiny rather than rent it from rivals.
