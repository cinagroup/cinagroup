## Top 7 Stories

### 1. Meta in Talks for $10 Billion Compute Deal with Anthropic

Meta is reportedly negotiating a landmark deal to lease massive computing power to Anthropic, in what could become a $10 billion arrangement, according to The New York Times. The deal would give Anthropic access to Meta's substantial GPU infrastructure as the AI lab races to train and deploy next-generation frontier models.

The potential partnership underscores the staggering compute demands of cutting-edge AI development. For Meta, the deal would generate significant revenue from its AI infrastructure investments while positioning the company as a key enabler of the broader AI ecosystem. For Anthropic, it secures the hardware runway needed to compete with OpenAI and Google without the multi-billion-dollar data center build-out those companies have undertaken. If finalized, this would be one of the largest compute-as-a-service deals in AI history.

### 2. Apple Sues OpenAI Over Trade Secrets, Alleges "Pattern of Theft"

Apple has filed a lawsuit against OpenAI, alleging the AI company engaged in a "pattern of theft" through former Apple employees now working at OpenAI, The Verge reports. Apple claims these employees improperly took proprietary information related to AI and machine learning technologies. The iPhone maker is reportedly also targeting dozens of individual OpenAI employees with legal letters, escalating the conflict beyond the corporate level.

The lawsuit marks a dramatic escalation in tensions between two of the most valuable technology companies in the world. Apple has been building its own AI capabilities, including on-device intelligence features, while OpenAI has aggressively recruited top AI talent. The case raises significant questions about trade secret enforcement in the hyper-competitive AI talent market, where engineers frequently move between companies and the line between generalized expertise and proprietary knowledge can blur. Legal experts suggest this could set important precedents for how courts handle AI-related intellectual property disputes.

### 3. Study: AI Advice Suppresses Willingness to Admit "I Don't Know"

A new study from researchers at French and Italian universities has found that access to AI advice dramatically suppresses people's willingness to admit ignorance — even when the AI is wrong. Published in a paper titled "AI advice suppresses people's willingness to say 'I don't know', even when the advice is wrong and accuracy is incentivized," the research found that the baseline rate of participants suspending judgment dropped from 44% to just 3% when AI advice was available.

More alarmingly, accuracy collapsed alongside the reduced skepticism. Only 9% of participants gave correct answers with AI help, compared to 27% without it — meaning people who would have been correct abandoned their own knowledge in favor of confident-sounding but incorrect AI outputs. Confidence, meanwhile, soared from 30% to 76%. The study used questions deliberately designed to trip up large language models, such as obscure visual details from films, and tested frontier models including GPT-5.5, Claude Sonnet 4.6, and Gemini 3.5 Flash, which all struggled with certain question types. The findings add urgency to calls for better AI literacy and skepticism training as these tools become ubiquitous.

### 4. Anthropic Migrates 1 Million Lines of Code in Under Two Weeks with Claude Code

Anthropic published a detailed case study on how its own engineers are using Claude Code to perform massive code migrations that were previously multi-year undertakings. Jarred Sumner, co-founder of Bun and Member of Technical Staff at Anthropic, used Claude Code to migrate the Bun runtime from Zig to Rust — producing one million lines of code in less than two weeks, with 100% of the existing test suite passing in CI before merge.

In a separate project, Mike Krieger, co-lead of Anthropic Labs, migrated a Python codebase to 165,000 lines of TypeScript over a single weekend, using hundreds of agents, eight phase gates, and three adversarial review rounds. Anthropic's approach shifts the paradigm: instead of fixing code, you fix the process loop that produces the code. The company argues that AI has fundamentally changed the economics of code migrations — the worst-case scenario is now simply deleting the branch and trying again, rather than losing quarters or years of engineering effort.

### 5. Netflix Paid $587 Million for Ben Affleck's AI Company

Netflix's acquisition of Ben Affleck's AI startup was valued at $587 million, according to a new report from The Hollywood Reporter. The deal, which closed earlier this year, gives Netflix ownership of AI technology developed by the actor-director's company, which focuses on AI-powered content creation and production tools.

The acquisition reflects Hollywood's accelerating embrace of AI technologies for film and television production. Netflix, which has been investing heavily in AI for content recommendation, dubbing, and visual effects, appears to be betting that AI-driven production tools can reduce costs and accelerate content pipelines. The $587 million price tag — a substantial sum even by Netflix's standards — signals that the streaming giant views AI production technology as strategically critical to maintaining its competitive edge in an increasingly crowded streaming market.

### 6. SpaceX in Talks to Provide Computing Power for Pentagon's AI Push

SpaceX is in discussions with the U.S. Department of Defense to supply computing infrastructure for the Pentagon's expanding artificial intelligence programs, The Wall Street Journal reports. The talks center on SpaceX's Starshield satellite network and ground-based computing capabilities that could support military AI applications ranging from autonomous systems to intelligence analysis.

The potential partnership highlights the growing intersection of commercial space technology and national security AI infrastructure. SpaceX's Starlink constellation already provides communications capabilities to military users, and extending into AI compute would mark a significant expansion of the company's defense business. For the Pentagon, tapping commercial providers like SpaceX offers a path to rapidly scale AI computing capacity without the lead times and costs of building dedicated government data centers. The discussions come as the U.S. military accelerates its AI adoption across all branches.

### 7. The Economist Warns: "When China's Open-Source AI Is a Trap"

The Economist published a provocative analysis warning that China's embrace of open-source AI models may serve strategic rather than altruistic purposes. The piece argues that Chinese AI labs — including those behind models like DeepSeek and Qwen — are releasing powerful open-weight models not just to compete with Western AI companies, but to shape global AI standards, build dependencies, and potentially embed surveillance capabilities.

The analysis has sparked debate in AI policy circles about the geopolitical dimensions of the open-source AI movement. While open-weight models from Chinese labs have been widely praised for democratizing access to cutting-edge AI capabilities, the piece urges Western policymakers and companies to consider the long-term strategic implications. The warning arrives as governments worldwide grapple with how to regulate AI while preserving the benefits of open research and development.
