### 1. OpenAI Publishes Solutions to More Than 370 Outstanding Math Challenges

OpenAI has published solutions to more than 370 outstanding mathematics challenges, including results tied to longstanding open problems such as Navier–Stokes. The release landed as a major flex of the company's reasoning models, but it also triggered an unusually pointed debate about credit and process.

Coverage from Fortune and Yahoo highlights that experts are demanding a voice in how the results are attributed and verified, with critics asking whether machine-generated proofs meet the bar of a peer-reviewed contribution. The episode captures a growing friction point: AI systems can now produce novel-looking results faster than the academic community can absorb or credit them.

### 4. Google Is Cutting Free Gemini Access Down to Flash-Lite

Google is removing free access to Gemini Flash and Pro and dropping the free tier to Flash-Lite, per The Verge and Android Authority. The change takes effect around October 9 and reshapes the economics of Google's consumer AI funnel.

For users on budget and Plus-style plans, the shift means tighter usage limits and access to less capable models. It reflects the pressure frontier AI providers face to balance free-tier generosity against the compute costs of serving their best models.

### 7. Debate Sharpens Over the Safety of Personal AI Agents

AI personal agents are "having a moment," with coverage from USA Today and MSN asking whether they are safe to use and cataloguing the agents that now live inside text messages. The products promise convenience by acting on a user's behalf across messaging and other channels.

The catch is trust: agents that can read messages, take actions, and hold sensitive context expand the attack surface for scams, prompt injection, and data exposure. Regulators are already taking notice, with guidance emerging on governing AI agents in government settings.
