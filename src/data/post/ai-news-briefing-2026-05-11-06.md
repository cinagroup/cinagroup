## AI News Briefing — May 11, 2026

### 7 Top Stories

**1. Cloudflare Lays Off 1,100 Workers as AI Usage Jumps 600%**

Cloudflare announced it is cutting approximately 1,100 employees, framing the move not as cost reduction but as a strategic pivot into the "agentic AI era." The company's AI-related traffic has surged 600%, fundamentally changing the nature of its infrastructure needs. CEO Matthew Prince characterized the restructuring as a redefinition of "how a world-class, high-growth company operates and creates value," sparking debate about whether AI adoption inherently requires massive workforce reductions in the tech sector.

**2. Meta Employees Report "Miserable" Conditions Amid AI Push and Looming Layoffs**

According to the New York Times, Meta staff are experiencing widespread anger and anxiety as the company tracks employee computer activity to train internal AI models while simultaneously planning to cut 10% of its workforce. Employees describe being pushed to build so many AI agents that management had to introduce "agents to find agents, and agents to rate agents." Some staff are actively seeking severance by signaling they want to be laid off, and others reportedly no longer view Meta as a place for a long-term career.

**3. OpenAI Launches Codex Chrome Extension for Browser-Based Task Automation**

OpenAI released a Chrome Web Store extension that lets Codex interact with websites and apps where users are already signed in. The extension operates in task-specific tab groups, allowing Codex to complete workflows — filling forms, navigating interfaces, and extracting data — without disrupting the user's active browsing session. This represents a significant step toward AI agents that can directly manipulate web applications on behalf of users, blurring the line between coding assistance and autonomous task execution.

**4. Musk v. Altman Trial Enters Final Week with Explosive Board Testimony**

The closely watched legal battle between Elon Musk and OpenAI entered its closing phase as former board member Helen Toner detailed the deliberations that led to Sam Altman's firing, revealing the board discussed merging OpenAI with Anthropic and considered Dario Amodei as potential CEO during the crisis. Former employees Rosie Campbell and Tasha McCauley testified via video deposition about alleged patterns of dishonesty at OpenAI, while legal experts questioned whether the proceedings have devolved into an Altman character trial rather than a case about Musk's withheld donations. Closing arguments are expected within a week.

**5. Maryland Fights $2B AI Data Center Power Grid Upgrade Costs**

Maryland has filed a complaint with federal energy regulators over a $2 billion power grid upgrade driven by out-of-state AI data center operators, arguing the costs violate ratepayer protection pledges. The state contends that AI infrastructure demand is forcing local residents to subsidize power grid expansions that primarily benefit external tech companies. This case highlights the growing tension between AI's massive energy requirements and the communities bearing the infrastructure burden — a conflict that is expected to play out across multiple states as data center construction accelerates.

**6. Mozilla Discloses 271 Firefox Bugs Found by Claude Mythos Preview**

Mozilla has published details on 271 security vulnerabilities identified in Firefox by Anthropic's Claude Mythos Preview, an unusually large haul from an AI-assisted security audit. The company made the calculated decision to unhide bug reports ahead of its typical multi-month disclosure window, citing "the extraordinary level of interest in this topic and the urgency of action needed throughout the software ecosystem." This raises both the promise of AI-driven security research and the question of how many undiscovered vulnerabilities exist across the broader software landscape.

**7. Google's Gmail "Help Me Write" Gets Tone Personalization and Drive Integration**

Google is rolling out an upgraded "Help me write" feature in Gmail that generates email drafts matching the user's personal writing style and tone. The enhanced tool can also pull relevant context from Google Drive and Gmail based on the user's prompt, effectively creating a contextual email assistant that knows your documents, your previous correspondence, and your communication style. This represents a deeper integration of AI into daily productivity workflows, moving beyond generic text generation toward personalized, context-aware assistance.
