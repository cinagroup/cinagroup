## Top 7 Stories

### 1. Anthropic "Pauses" Token-Based Billing for Claude Agent SDK

Anthropic has quietly paused the token-based billing model for its Claude Agent SDK, following significant developer pushback since its introduction. The move comes after weeks of complaints from builders who said the unpredictable cost structure made it nearly impossible to budget for agentic applications at scale.

The pause signals a broader industry reckoning around how to price AI agent workloads. Unlike traditional API calls, agent-based workflows can involve dozens or hundreds of model invocations per task, making per-token pricing particularly volatile. Anthropic is expected to announce a revised pricing structure in the coming weeks.

### 2. OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google Join World Leaders at G7 AI Summit

In an unprecedented move, leaders from OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google were invited to participate in the G7 summit alongside heads of state. The discussions centered on AI governance frameworks, cross-border data regulations, and the establishment of international safety standards for frontier models.

The summit produced a joint communiqué calling for voluntary commitments on model evaluation transparency and a proposed international AI safety board. Industry observers note this marks the first time all three major frontier labs have been at the same table for policy discussions of this magnitude.

### 3. Google Unveils Strategy to "Win the AI War"

Google has laid out an aggressive multi-pronged strategy to reclaim its position at the forefront of the AI race. The plan includes massive infrastructure investment, a revamped Gemini model family, and deeper integration of AI across its entire product suite from Search to Workspace.

Internal documents suggest Google is particularly focused on closing the gap with Anthropic in the enterprise coding assistant market and with OpenAI in consumer-facing AI products. The company is also reportedly exploring strategic acquisitions to bolster its AI talent pool.

### 4. Meta Launches Muse Model to Rival Frontier Labs

Meta has officially debuted its new Muse model, positioning it as a direct competitor to offerings from Google, OpenAI, and Anthropic. The model reportedly excels at multimodal reasoning and creative tasks, with early benchmarks showing competitive performance against GPT-4-class models.

True to Meta's open-source philosophy, Muse will be released under a permissive license, continuing the company's strategy of democratizing access to frontier AI capabilities. The move is expected to intensify competition in the open-weight model space and put pressure on closed-source providers.

### 5. Claude Managed Agents Gain "Dreaming" Memory Preservation

Anthropic has introduced a novel "dreaming" capability for Claude Managed Agents that allows them to consolidate and preserve memories across sessions. The feature addresses one of the key limitations of current AI agents — their inability to maintain persistent context over long time horizons.

The dreaming process runs during idle periods, compressing and organizing interaction histories into structured memory stores. Early adopters report significant improvements in agent coherence for long-running projects, with some describing it as a step toward more autonomous AI collaborators.

### 6. Pentagon-Anthropic Standoff Creates Dilemma for Other AI Labs

The ongoing dispute between the Pentagon and Anthropic over defense contracts continues to send shockwaves through the AI industry. The standoff has forced other major labs to confront uncomfortable questions about their own relationships with military and intelligence agencies.

Several labs are reportedly revising their acceptable use policies in response, while others are accelerating efforts to build technical safeguards that would prevent military applications of their models. The situation highlights the growing tension between commercial opportunities and ethical constraints in the AI sector.

### 7. Circus Chief: Unified Mobile Interface for Claude Code, Codex, and Gemini

A new Show HN project called Circus Chief has gained traction by offering a unified mobile interface for managing Claude Code, OpenAI Codex, and Google Gemini agents from a single app. The tool aims to simplify the increasingly fragmented landscape of AI coding assistants.

Developer feedback has been largely positive, with users praising the streamlined workflow for switching between different AI backends depending on the task. The project underscores a growing demand for agent orchestration tools as developers juggle multiple AI services in their daily workflows.
