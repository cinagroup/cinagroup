## Top 7 Stories

1. **OpenAI Ships GPT-5.6 in Kiro, Targeting Developer Price-Performance.** OpenAI announced GPT-5.6 availability in Kiro, its developer platform, with a focus on improving price-performance for developers. The release signals OpenAI's push to defend its developer ecosystem against cheaper open-weight and rival frontier models, with new tiers aimed at making frontier inference more economical at scale.

2. **NVIDIA Faces Growth Test as Rubin Debut Meets AI Financing Scrutiny.** Reuters reports NVIDIA enters a critical quarter as its next-gen Rubin architecture begins its debut amid growing investor questions about how AI infrastructure is financed. Concerns over concentration risk — a handful of hyperscalers funding massive GPU orders — are putting pressure on NVIDIA's growth narrative heading into earnings.

3. **Anthropic Tells SF Staff to Work From Home Amid Possible Security-Team Strike.** Business Insider reports Anthropic instructed San Francisco employees to work from home due to a possible strike by its security team. The labor action highlights growing tensions inside frontier AI labs over safety staffing, pay, and working conditions as security teams take on expanded responsibility for model and infrastructure protection.

4. **US Charges NVIDIA and Supermicro Employees Over AI Server Exports to China.** Prosecutors in the US and Taiwan have charged individuals, including NVIDIA and Super Micro staffers, over allegedly illegal exports of AI servers to China. The cases underscore tightening export-control enforcement around advanced AI hardware and the legal exposure of employees facilitating restricted shipments.

5. **Big Tech's Frantic Race to Quell Growing AI Backlash.** The Wall Street Journal details how major tech companies are scrambling to respond to rising public and political backlash against AI — from job-displacement fears to data-privacy concerns. The report describes coordinated messaging, policy outreach, and product guardrails as companies try to protect the AI buildout from regulatory and reputational damage.

6. **Why Irregular's AI Tests for Meta, Anthropic and OpenAI Went Off the Rails.** The New York Times examines how safety evaluations run by Irregular — a startup that stress-tests frontier models — produced erratic results when testing Meta, Anthropic, and OpenAI systems. The episode underscores how fragile current AI evaluation methods remain and fuels the debate over whether the industry's safety-testing infrastructure is ready for high-stakes deployment decisions.

7. **SpaceXAI Adopts NVIDIA Vera CPU to Scale Agentic AI.** NVIDIA announced that SpaceXAI is deploying its Vera CPU at massive scale to accelerate agentic AI workloads. The deal underscores the shift toward CPU-GPU co-design as agentic systems — which run heavy orchestration, tool-calling, and reasoning loops — become a primary driver of new data-center infrastructure demand.
