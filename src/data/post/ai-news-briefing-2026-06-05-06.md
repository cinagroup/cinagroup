## Top 7 Stories

### 1. Meta Steals Tesla's Playbook — Building Data Centers in Tents

Meta is adopting a strategy pioneered by Tesla to dramatically cut data center construction costs: building them inside large industrial tents instead of traditional permanent structures. The approach could slash both construction timelines and capital expenditure as Meta races to build out AI infrastructure to support its growing model training and inference workloads.

Tent-based data centers offer a modular, rapidly deployable alternative that can be scaled up or relocated as needed. This reflects a broader industry shift toward flexible infrastructure as hyperscalers compete to meet surging AI compute demand. The move underscores how cost pressure is driving even the largest tech companies to rethink conventional data center design.

### 2. Apple Approves Poke as the First AI Agent on Messages for Business

Poke, a startup that enables users to interact with AI agents through simple text messages, has become the first AI agent approved for Apple's Messages for Business platform. This milestone marks Apple's cautious but growing openness to AI agents operating within its ecosystem, a significant shift for a company historically protective of its platform boundaries.

The approval signals that Apple is beginning to carve out a lane for AI-driven customer interactions on its native messaging platform. Businesses will be able to deploy AI agents that handle customer inquiries, bookings, and transactions directly within iMessage, expanding the reach of conversational AI beyond standalone apps and web interfaces.

### 3. Helion Raises $465M to Build Fusion Power Plant for Microsoft

Helion Energy, the fusion startup backed by Sam Altman, has raised $465 million in new funding to accelerate construction of its first commercial power plant, which it plans to deliver to Microsoft by 2028. The massive capital infusion comes as the race to achieve commercially viable fusion energy intensifies, with AI compute demand driving unprecedented interest in clean, baseload power.

The Helion-Microsoft partnership is one of the most ambitious energy deals in tech history. If successful, the fusion plant would provide carbon-free, virtually limitless electricity to power Microsoft's data centers — a critical need as AI workloads push power grids to their limits. The round highlights how the AI infrastructure boom is catalyzing investment in next-generation energy technologies.

### 4. Meta Launches AI Creator Assistant on Facebook

Meta has rolled out a new AI-powered creator assistant on Facebook, designed to help content creators navigate performance metrics and audience engagement without sifting through complex dashboards. Creators can now ask natural-language questions like "When should I post?" and "What are people saying in my comments?" to get instant, actionable insights.

The tool represents Meta's broader push to embed AI across its creator tools, making the platform more accessible to everyday content producers. By democratizing access to analytics, Meta aims to boost creator engagement and retention on Facebook as competition from TikTok and YouTube intensifies.

### 5. Critical Vulnerability Threatens Millions of AI Agents

Ars Technica reports that a critical security vulnerability in a widely used open-source package is putting millions of AI agents at risk. The flaw could allow attackers to manipulate or compromise autonomous AI systems that depend on the affected library for core functionality.

The discovery highlights a growing security challenge as AI agents proliferate across enterprises and consumer applications. Many agent frameworks share common dependencies, creating systemic risk where a single vulnerability can cascade across millions of deployments. The incident underscores the urgent need for robust security practices in the rapidly evolving AI agent ecosystem.

### 6. WWDC 2026 Preview: Siri Revamp and Apple Intelligence Updates

With Apple's Worldwide Developers Conference approaching, expectations are building for a major overhaul of Siri powered by advanced large language models, along with significant updates to the Apple Intelligence suite. Industry observers anticipate new capabilities that would make Siri far more conversational and contextually aware.

WWDC 2026 is shaping up to be one of Apple's most consequential developer events in recent years, as the company works to catch up with competitors who have already deployed advanced AI assistants. The updates will signal how seriously Apple is taking the AI race and whether its on-device-first approach can compete with cloud-based alternatives from Google, OpenAI, and Anthropic.

### 7. NSA Reportedly Using Anthropic's Mythos for Cyber Operations

According to a Financial Times report, the U.S. National Security Agency is leveraging Anthropic's open-source vulnerability discovery framework, Mythos, for cyber operations. The tool, originally released by Anthropic to help developers find and patch security flaws in their code, is now being deployed for offensive cybersecurity purposes.

The report raises complex questions about the dual-use nature of AI security tools. While Anthropic released Mythos with the intention of improving software safety across the industry, its capabilities for automated vulnerability discovery make it attractive to intelligence agencies. The story highlights the growing tension between open-source AI safety research and its potential applications in state-level cyber operations.
