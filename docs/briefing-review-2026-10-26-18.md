1. **OpenAI frontier training without live internet — supported for a bounded scope.** [OpenAI's October 23 description](https://openai.com/index/securing-our-frontier-model-training-environment/) says its frontier model training policy excludes live internet for now, describes a September 20 DNS misconfiguration and a resulting pause, and says frontier reinforcement learning and internal inference resumed October 1. The policy is not a permanent ban or a statement about every model and every product. The separate Anthropic October 9 comparison and quoted former employee need their own primary sources.
2. **Anthropic listing preparation — supported as preparation, not completion.** [Reuters on September 28](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/exclusiveanthropic-warns-ai-may-pose-existential-risks-to-humanity-in-ipo-filing-4921447) reported a prospectus reviewed by Reuters. Anthropic's [public SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm) is dated October 26; its exact availability relative to this issue's cutoff must be established before using it as contemporaneous evidence. Neither a listing nor its final value was guaranteed. Any OpenAI IPO timetable remains a rumor absent company confirmation.
3. **Apollo economist on AI returns — accurately attributed opinion, not universal proof.** [Apollo's October 24 note](https://www.apollo.com/wealth/insights-news/insights/daily-spark/no-signs-of-ai-payoff-outside-the-tech-sector) shows Torsten Slok's assessment based on aggregate revenue per worker, margins for the S&P 493 versus the largest technology companies, and productivity data. These aggregate associations do not independently establish that no individual non-tech company benefits or isolate AI's causal effect; the data cover large listed US companies, not every business.
4. **Antitrust lawsuit — allegation supported, “new” misdated.** [AP on September 19](https://apnews.com/article/960af4308161eaf4ed13c383b0ce1c1b) reports a September 18 proposed class action against Anthropic, OpenAI, SpaceXAI and Google. The complaint alleges an agreement; it does not establish collusion or a legal violation. The suit was more than a month old at this issue's cutoff; its later procedural status and responses were not checked.
5. **OpenAI models on Google Cloud and Antigravity — availability overstated.** [Google's October 21 announcement](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/google-cloud-brings-openai-models-to-customers) describes a private preview for selected enterprise customers on Google Cloud and local Codex integration. It says OpenAI models in Antigravity would come later, not that the entire 6 and 6.1 suite was already broadly available there. Microsoft relationship and multi-cloud effects require their own documented scope.
6. **EPA citizen-suit ruling — supported for a specific case, not all data-center litigation.** [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/10/judge-rejects-trumps-wild-claim-epa-can-dismiss-data-center-pollution-lawsuits/) and [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/us-law-week/trump-epa-can-join-xai-data-center-suit-fails-to-win-dismissal) report a judge rejected a unilateral dismissal theory in an NAACP Clean Air Act case concerning a SpaceXAI/xAI data-center project, while allowing EPA intervention. This procedural ruling is not a finding that pollution allegations have been proved. Obtain the actual order and current docket before definitive legal characterization.
7. **Meta commercial terms — wording change reported; operational impact unverified.** [Ars Technica's October 20 report](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/10/meta-drops-the-word-train-from-its-commercial-tos-after-ai-backlash/) says Meta removed the word “train” from proposed commercial terms amid criticism. It does not prove either that data practices did or did not change; compare the exact versions and implementation. The proposal covered business-site content, which is distinct from every customer's personal data. Effective date and subsequent revisions need cutoff-specific verification.
